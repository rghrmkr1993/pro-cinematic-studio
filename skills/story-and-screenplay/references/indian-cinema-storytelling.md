# Indian cinema storytelling (techniques)

Described as craft techniques seen across Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam, Kannada and Hindi popular cinema. No specific films, scripts, lines or lyrics are named or reproduced; build originals. These are conventions and tendencies, not rules; mix or break them on purpose.

## 1. Shared traits
- Emotion is the product: the audience expects strong feeling (love, pride, grief, humour, devotion) in every story, often in one film.
- Family is the arena: parents, siblings, in-laws and community press on the individual; the central choice is often duty versus desire.
- Social themes ride inside entertainment: caste, class, land, honour, corruption, education, women's agency, migration. Treat them with specificity and care; no caricature.
- Blended tone: comedy, romance, action and melodrama can share one story; the tone shifts are managed with music and cuts.
- Music is part of the storytelling: songs and background score carry feeling and time passing.
- Ensemble: a hero, a heroine, a comic or friend, a mentor or elder, a villain with local power. Supporting characters get memorable habits.
- Three-act plus interval: the interval is a structural pivot (section 4).

## 2. Hero intro (mass entry)
A technique to establish who the hero is, before he speaks, in one staged moment.
1. Build anticipation: other characters talk about him, a crowd waits, an object (footwear, vehicle, a shadow) appears before the face.
2. Show the competence or the code: he solves a problem, protects someone, or stays calm in a threat.
3. The reveal frame: low angle, slow motion, light and wind on cloth and hair, a signature gesture or prop (a turned collar, a lit match, a lifted towel).
4. Sound: a drop in ambience, then a theme motif and a weighty beat; a one-line statement of his code or humour.
5. Payoff: the crowd reacts; the audience learns what he will fight for.
For an AI scene: one strong image, one gesture, one reaction shot. Keep it under 8 s per shot. The heroine's entry uses the same grammar with grace, wit or quiet strength, not only beauty.

## 3. Mass moments and punch beats
- A mass moment is a set-up, a delay, a release: a threat grows, the audience waits, the hero acts, the crowd cheers.
- Elements: a clear injustice, a held pause, a reversal of power, a short line, a visual flourish.
- Place 3 to 5 across a feature; one per short sequence. Do not spend them early.
- Quiet mass: a gentle character stops a loud one with one sentence or one look.

## 4. Interval block
- A film halves around 60 to 70 minutes. The interval point is a high turn: a reveal, a betrayal, a death, a vow, a villain's win, or the hero's identity coming out.
- Technique: Act 1 and half of Act 2 build the want; the interval image changes the question for the second half ("will he win" becomes "can he survive the truth").
- The block lands as a cliffhanger frame with a sound hit and a held shot; the second half opens with a consequence, a time jump or a changed status.
- For a short: use one mid-point flip at about 50 percent.

## 5. Songs as narrative
- Song as scene: a song replaces a scene when words are not enough; it must change the situation by the end (strangers to lovers, poor to prepared, fear to resolve).
- Placement: introduction song (hero or place, around 10 to 15 percent), romance song (end of Act 1 or in the first half of Act 2), celebration or festival song, a montage song (training, preparation, passing time), a sorrow song before the low point, an item or dance set-piece for energy, a climax or end-credit song.
- Writing it: write the story of the song in 4 to 6 image beats (a lyrics-free storyboard) before any music is made. Each beat has a location, costume or colour change and an emotion step.
- Dream and fantasy locations are a convention: the song may leave the real world to show a feeling. Mark it as a visual choice.
- Original lyrics and music only; no lyrics reproduced. See `../../music-and-sound/SKILL.md` for the sound side.
- For AI video: treat each song beat as a 4 to 8 s shot, keep character looks locked by reference, and keep choreography simple (walks, turns, lifted hands, light play) since complex dance breaks.

## 6. Genre conventions
| Genre or tradition | Tendencies |
|---|---|
| Tamil mass and rural drama | Strong hero code, local honour, land and family feud, rooted setting, folk music, crowd reactions, punch dialogue, themes of dignity |
| Tamil realist and urban | Observed detail, restrained music, slow burn, class and work, moral grey zones, long takes |
| Telugu mass and family entertainer | Big scale, hero build-ups, comedic tracks, faction or family rivalry, festivals, high-energy songs, emotional sentiment of mother or brother |
| Malayalam realist | Naturalism, small town detail, domestic space, wry humour, ambiguity, ordinary-person stakes, understated performance |
| Kannada | Mix of rooted rural, coastal myth and big-scale action; folk elements; pride of place |
| Hindi mainstream | Romance and family, urban youth, ensemble comedy, patriotic and social dramas, big-hearted melodrama, wedding and festival set pieces |
| Devotional and mythic | Faith, sacrifice, divine intervention, symbolism; sensitive to religious feeling, so consult the client |
| Thriller and crime | Procedural turns, moral choice, an interval twist, a final reveal |
| Comedy | Situational chaos, mistaken identity, family farce; the straight man and the schemer |

## 7. Romance conventions
- Barriers: class, caste, community, family approval, distance, education, timing. Love is shown through glances, small gifts, shared tasks and stolen moments; physical contact is restrained and loaded.
- Meet-cute in everyday places: bus stop, temple, tea shop, college corridor, rain shelter, well, festival crowd.
- Courtship beats: notice, tease, small help, separation, confession, family test.
- Rain, flowers, mirrors, bangles, anklets, hair, saree pallu and lamps are common symbols; use fresh variations.

## 8. Climax and resolution
- The climax gathers the community: the whole village, family or office is present; the hero's choice is public.
- The fight or confrontation is the argument of the film in action; it ends with an emotional release, not only a win.
- Resolution: reconciliation with family, a speech that is short and specific, a final image of home, a festival or a road.
- Sacrifice and reunion are strong closing beats; an ambiguous ending is more common in realist traditions.

## 9. Family and social themes (handled with care)
- Parent-child: expectation versus ambition, sacrifice, silent love.
- Siblings and in-laws: loyalty, money, inheritance, reconciliation.
- Women's agency: education, work, choosing a partner; avoid rescue clichés; give her action.
- Class and work: dignity of labour, migration to the city, debt.
- Village and city: tradition versus change, language pride.
- Always check the setting detail (kinship terms, food, dress, ritual) with `../../asian-human-reference/SKILL.md` and `../../indic-languages/SKILL.md`, and flag religious, caste or political content for the creative-director and the client.

## 10. Adapting to short and ad forms
- Compress the grammar: one entrance image, one beat of feeling, one punch frame. A 15 s ad can hold: build-up (shadow, prop), reveal (low angle), reaction, product or message.
- Songs become 6 to 15 s montage beats, choreographed by cuts and light, not dance.
- Family warmth sells: a shared meal, a parent's hands, a festival kitchen. Keep claims out and feeling in.
