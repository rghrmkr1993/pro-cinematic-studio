# Regions and appearance ranges

Labels: [E] = estimate from general anthropology and photographic knowledge, [V] = verified in a cited source (see sources.md), [U] = unverified, test before relying.
Everything below is a RANGE. Regions overlap, populations have mixed for millennia, and individuals differ. Pick one concrete point per slot per person and vary it across a group. Never infer religion, caste or nationality from a look.

## How to use skin descriptors

- Describe colour + undertone + light behaviour: "medium golden-brown skin, warm olive undertone, soft natural sheen on cheekbones, visible pores".
- Undertone words: warm (yellow, golden, peach), olive (green-gold), neutral, cool-red (rosy, ruddy), deep (rich brown or espresso, with red, bronze or blue-black cast).
- Monk (10-point, 2023) and Fitzpatrick (6 types, 1975) are measurement aids [V]. Fitzpatrick was built to predict sunburn in light skin and is criticised as poorly predictive of skin tone [V]. Use Monk swatches in QA, never as a prompt word alone.
- Skin varies within one person: sun-exposed hands and neck are darker, knuckles and under-eye deeper, lips and palms lighter. Say "natural uneven tone".
- Tone ranges are broad and independent of region: every region below contains people lighter and deeper than the typical range shown [E].

Light eyes occur but are uncommon outside parts of the north and Kashmir [E]. Freckles, moles, acne scars and vitiligo are real and can appear, treated respectfully.

## Face-shape vocabulary (any region)

Oval, round, long, square, heart, diamond, broad, narrow. Add: high or low cheekbones, strong or soft jaw, broad or narrow forehead, thick or thin brows, deep-set or prominent eyes, visible lines, asymmetry (no real face is symmetric).

## Eye vocabulary

- Monolid: smooth upper lid, no visible crease; lashes may point down.
- Hooded: skin fold partly covers the lid; crease partly hidden.
- Double lid: visible crease, narrow (tapered) or wide (parallel).
- Inner epicanthic fold: skin over the inner corner; common in East, Southeast and hill-Northeast peoples, also appears elsewhere [V partly].
- Also: deep-set, round, almond, upturned, downturned, wide-set, close-set, heavy lids, thick lashes.
- Eye colour: dark brown to black is the norm; light brown common; hazel, green or grey rare but real in Kashmir and north India [E].

## Hair vocabulary

Straight, wavy, curly, coily (coily is rare outside Aeta, Papuan and some South Indian and Andaman groups) [E]; fine to coarse; thick to thin; receding hairlines, greys, side or centre parting, frizz and flyaways (real hair has them).

## DO NOT descriptors

- "Fair", "fairer", "porcelain", "snow-white", "radiant", "luminous", "flawless" as default skin words.
- "Exotic", "oriental", "mystical", "ethnic", "tribal" as a label, "Asian features", "slanted eyes", "almond-shaped eyes" as the only eye description.
- "Typical Indian / Chinese / Japanese face".
- Skin colour as a proxy for caste, class or morality (dark skin for poor or villain, fair skin for rich or hero).
- Same face for everyone ("pan-Asian generic": small nose, thin lips, smooth lighter skin).
- Western-style tall double lids on every East Asian character, or eyes forced to a squint.
- Skin lightened or greyed under cool-white light; oversmoothed or airbrushed skin.
- Mixing: a Japanese character in Chinese dress, Thai in Khmer costume, Punjabi in Kerala dress, without a story reason.
- "Brownface" tint to change a character's ethnicity; "brown skin" as a catch-all for South Asian, Middle Eastern and Latino.
