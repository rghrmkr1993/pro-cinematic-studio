# Heritage instruments (Tamil, Carnatic and South Indian folk)

Extends `instruments.md` section 1 (one-line table); do not repeat it. Labels: F sourced in session research 2026-10-14 (`sources.md` section "2026-10-14 additions"), E craft knowledge, U unverified or sources differ. EQ and pan values are starting points (E); trust the ear. Sizes and tunings vary by maker. For hero cues record a real player; AI renders flatten gamakas and mis-tune microtones (U). Never name a player in a prompt.
Mix zones used below: sub 20-60 Hz, bass 60-200, low-mid 200-500, mid 500-2k, presence 2-5k, air 8k+.

## A. Concert (Carnatic) instruments

### Ghatam
- Build: baked clay pot with a narrow mouth turned out into a ridge; clay with brass or copper and small iron filings; size sets pitch (F).
- Technique: pot on lap, mouth to the belly; fingers, thumbs, palms and heels strike the outside; moving the mouth against the belly changes tone; neck strokes for ringing notes (F).
- Pitch: tuned to the singer's Sa by size; one pot per tonic (F, E). Sound: earthy, hollow, woody-ringing, with a metallic ping on the shoulder.
- Context: Carnatic concert, thani avartanam, fusion; pairs with mridangam, kanjira, morsing. Emotion: earthy, playful, dry humour, driving.
- Prompt: "ghatam, earthy clay-pot tones, tuned to Sa, fast finger rolls". Mix: HPF 80 Hz; body 150-300 Hz; ring 1-3 kHz; pan slightly right; short room, no long reverb. Care: fragile; neutral.

### Mridangam
- Build: hollow jackfruit-wood barrel, two heads: a right treble head with a black tuning paste (soru) and a left bass head, laced with leather straps and wooden dowels (F, E).
- Technique: fingers and palms, strokes named by syllable (tha, thi, thom, nam, dhin, gu). Right head tuned to Sa; left head bends with the heel for bass glides (F).
- Pitch: tonic-tuned; low-mid boom plus bright ring. Sound: deep, round, crisp, "talking".
- Context: Carnatic vocal and instrumental main percussion, dance, temple. Pairs with ghatam, kanjira, morsing, violin, voice. Emotion: grounded, festive, precise.
- Prompt: "mridangam, adi tala pattern, crisp finger strokes, tuned to Sa, warm bass bends". Mix: body 100-200 Hz, slap 2-4 kHz; centre or just left of centre; light compression, short hall.

### Kanjira
- Build: single frame drum, jackfruit wood ring about 17 to 19 cm, one head (traditionally monitor-lizard skin, now goat or synthetic), metal jingle discs in the frame (F).
- Technique: right palm and fingers on the head, left hand supports and presses near the rim to bend pitch; water on the head lowers pitch; untuned and very high otherwise (F).
- Sound: bright, snappy, jingling, fast. Context: thani, fast korvai, with mridangam. Emotion: sparkle, urgency, wit.
- Prompt: "kanjira, bright slaps, fast patterns, jingle". Mix: HPF 150 Hz, presence 3-6 kHz, pan right; de-ess jingles.

### Morsing (mouth harp)
- Build: metal frame with a steel tongue (reed) held to the teeth; plucked with a finger (F).
- Technique: mouth shape and breath create pitch; high breath control; players keep different morsings for different tonics (E).
- Sound: buzzing, twanging, rubbery rhythm with an overtone melody. Context: thani with mridangam and ghatam. Emotion: playful, quirky, rhythmic colour.
- Prompt: "morsing, twangy mouth-harp rhythm, nasal overtones". Mix: low level, 800 Hz to 3 kHz, pan left of centre, no wide reverb.

### Violin (Carnatic)
- Build: standard violin, tuned relative to the singer's Sa; open strings Sa, Pa, Sa, Pa (lower Sa, lower Pa, Sa, Pa), e.g. C-G-C-G, in some schools Pa-Sa-Pa-Sa (F; the order differs by school, U).
- Posture: player sits cross-legged; the scroll rests on the right ankle bone, instrument braced against the chest; left hand free to glide for gamaka (F).
- Technique: long unbroken bow strokes echoing the breath, pressure bowing with left-hand slide for kampita; staccato avoided (F). Range roughly G3 to E6 in use (E).
- Sound: vocal, sliding, slightly nasal-warm. Context: follows or answers voice in concert (violin plays after each line); also solo concert, film. Emotion: yearning, human.
- Prompt: "Carnatic violin, floor-seated style, long bows, sliding gamakas, vocal-like, tuned to Sa". Mix: body 200-400 Hz, presence 2-4 kHz, pan slightly left of the singer, little reverb. Distinguish from Western vibrato.

### Veena (Saraswati veena)
- Build: carved jackfruit-wood body with a gourd resonator, 24 metal frets set in wax, four playing strings tuned around Sa and Pa and three side strings for drone and tala (E).
- Technique: right index and middle fingers pluck (mizrab-style); left fingers pull the string sideways across frets for gamakas; range about three and a half octaves (E).
- Sound: warm, resonant, sustained plucked with sympathetic bloom. Context: concert, temple, sacred home. Emotion: dignified, devotional, calm.
- Prompt: "Saraswati veena, warm plucked gamakas, soft sustain, devotional". Mix: body 100-300 Hz, pick 2-3 kHz, near centre, hall reverb 1.5 s.

### Flute (venu) and bansuri
- Build: bamboo side-blown flute; Carnatic venu has eight finger-holes, fingered with finger-pads; Hindustani bansuri has six holes, finger-tips (E). Sizes set key: larger = lower. Range about two octaves (E).
- Technique: breath control, tonguing, gamaka slides by half-covering holes and finger rolls; circular breathing on long phrases (E).
- Sound: breathy, airy, pastoral, sweet. Context: concert, film, folk, Krishna imagery, dawn. Pairs with tambura, mridangam, veena. Emotion: tender, longing, peace.
- Prompt: "bamboo venu flute, breathy long phrases, slow gamakas, slight finger slides, tambura drone". Mix: HPF 150 Hz, body 400 Hz to 1 kHz, air 6-10 kHz; centre; moderate reverb. Note: bansuri is Hindustani, venu is Carnatic; do not mix them casually.

### Tambura (tanpura)
- Build: long-necked plucked drone, resonant gourd, 4 strings, with threads (jivari) under strings to give a shimmering buzz (E).
- Tuning: Pa-Sa-Sa-Sa (low) or Ma-Sa-Sa-Sa (E); the buzz makes overtones bloom. Technique: slow circular plucking, never fretted.
- Sound: shimmering continuous bed, rich overtones. Context: all classical vocal and instrumental. Emotion: spiritual calm, grounding.
- Prompt: "tambura drone on Sa and Pa, warm, continuous, shimmering overtones". Mix: wide stereo or centred bed, -20 dB under lead, HPF 60 Hz, soft saturation; loop with crossfade. Electronic shruti apps are fine for guide, not for finals.

### Sruti box
- Build: wooden box with brass reeds (or electronic); a bellows (hand pump or electric) feeds air across reeds; knobs open reeds for Sa, Pa, Ma, Ni (E).
- Use: provides the drone for singing and instruments, esp. in small ensembles and practice; more portable than tambura.
- Sound: steady, reedy, nasal, slightly buzzy, no pluck decay. Emotion: steady anchor, devotional.
- Prompt: "shruti box drone on Sa, reedy sustained, steady pump". Mix: -22 dB, narrow EQ 200 Hz to 2 kHz, centre, gentle roll-off; check pitch against the lead, drone must be in tune (+/-5 cents, E).

### Harmonium (and the debate)
- Build: hand-pumped reed organ with keyboard, bellows pumped with the left hand, brass free reeds (E).
- Pitch: fixed equal temperament; cannot bend notes; can play the drone and chords.
- Debate: harmonium was banned from All India Radio from 1940 to 1971 because critics argued its fixed pitches cannot render microtones and gamaka, and fixed pitch conflicts with svara as a range; the debate had political and nationalist layers, and the ban was later relaxed (F, mainly Hindustani; the Carnatic position is less documented, U). Many Carnatic vocalists avoid it for concerts and use violin, tambura and sruti box; it is common in bhajan, Harikatha, therukoothu and Western-influenced film.
- Sound: breathy, nasal, reedy, warm. Emotion: devotional, folk narration.
- Prompt: "harmonium, soft sustained chords, bhajan accompaniment, hand-pumped". Mix: 200 Hz to 2 kHz, centre, low reverb. Authenticity note: avoid it on a serious Carnatic concert cue.

## B. Temple, wedding and processional

### Nadaswaram
- Build: long conical double-reed wind with a wooden body (aacha, ebony, sometimes sandalwood or metal), a double reed (seevali) in a metal staple, seven finger holes and five wax-sealed bottom holes (brahma-swaram) to adjust pitch, and a flaring bell (anasu) (F). Bari version about 34.5 inches and low (reported base about D, 2 kattai); shorter timiri is higher (F, U on values).
- Technique: strong breath, circular breathing, long phrases, heavy gamaka. Played in pairs, with a sruti (ottu) holding the drone, accompanied by a thavil pair and talam cymbals (F).
- Pitch/sound: loud, bright, piercing, outdoors, clarion. Context: temples, weddings (mangala vadyam: auspicious music), processions; periya melam (F). Emotion: joy, auspiciousness, grandeur.
- Cultural note: part of the temple and wedding ritual; also used at funeral urumi melam in some contexts, so match context (F).
- Prompt: "nadaswaram, bright piercing double-reed melody, gamaka-rich, with thavil, temple festival". Mix: HPF 120 Hz, body 500-900 Hz, tame 2-4 kHz harshness with a dynamic EQ, centre, big open reverb; keep level managed (very loud transients).

### Thavil
- Build: barrel-shaped double-headed drum; right head struck with a stick on the treble head, left head played with a hand wrapped in a finger-sheath (F).
- Technique: stick and fingers; a hard, rolling attack; played in pairs with the nadaswaram (F). Tuned with paste; very loud.
- Sound: sharp, thunderous, crisp, loud, with a metallic ring. Context: temple, wedding, procession; Thanjavur and the Carnatic tradition (F). Emotion: ceremony, power.
- Prompt: "thavil, hard crisp strokes, roll, temple festival". Mix: body 100-250 Hz, snap 3-5 kHz, pan wide-left or centre; compress; reverb in a stone-temple tail.

### Cymbals: talam, jalra, ilathalam
- Build: pairs of bronze cymbals; jalra (flat), ilathalam (small, pairs, joined by a cord), talam (small hand cymbals for tala keeping) (E).
- Technique: tala-keeping, keeps beats and sub-beats at the periya melam, ilathalam in Kerala-style temple (E, U on regional naming).
- Sound: bright metallic ring, shimmering. Context: periya melam, bhajan, temple procession. Emotion: devotion, pulse.
- Prompt: "bronze hand cymbals, tala keeping, bright ring". Mix: HPF 400 Hz, air 6-12 kHz, pan sides; sparingly.

### Chenda
- Build: cylindrical wooden drum with skin on both heads, struck with sticks; strongly Kerala (F). Used in temple festival (pooram, panchavadyam) (F), also in Tamil border regions (U).
- Sound: thundering, sharp, rolling. Emotion: ecstatic festival, power.
- Prompt: "chenda drums, thunderous rolls, temple pooram energy". Mix: HPF 100 Hz, 1-3 kHz attack; wide reverb. Say "Kerala" if regionally relevant.

### Kombu
- Build: C-shaped curved brass or copper natural horn (F). Used in processions, Pandi melam, Panchavadya (F).
- Sound: raw, bold fanfare, few notes. Emotion: announcement, ritual call.
- Prompt: "kombu horn, curved brass, ceremonial calls". Mix: 300 Hz to 3 kHz, distant reverb, pan off-centre.

## C. Folk and ritual (see cultural care first)

### Parai
- Build: single-sided frame drum, wooden ring with cow-skin stretched over one side, struck with two unequal sticks (F). Older uses include announcing news and orders since the Sangam period (F, U on scholarly detail).
- Technique: loud, sharp strokes with a thin stick and a thick stick, fast rolls, heated over fire to tighten skin (E). Parai attam is the dance form.
- Pitch/sound: high, raw, cutting, kinetic. Context: village festival, folk dance, funerals; film kuthu and gaana; pairs with thappu, urumi, pambai in folk melams (F).
- Emotion: raw energy, defiance, grief, celebration depending on context.
- Cultural note (important, F): parai is tied to Dalit and Paraiyar communities; colonial and caste hierarchy confined it to funerals, and revival groups now insist on playing it at all functions as pride. Avoid using it as exotic colour or as a caricature of a caste; credit and pay real players; avoid stereotyping scenes. Etymology and origin accounts differ (U).
- Prompt: "parai drum, raw sharp stick hits, fast rolls, village festival, celebratory". Mix: presence 2-5 kHz, HPF 100 Hz, limiter on transients, wide stereo with a room; avoid over-polished tuning.

### Thappu (and thappattam)
- Build: round frame drum with skin on one side, played with sticks or hands, used by Dalit communities in thappattam dance; some tied to Amman festivals (F). A Kerala padayani thappu differs (F).
- Sound: crisp, dry, tight, rhythmic, higher than parai. Emotion: street party, procession, ritual. Prompt: "thappu frame drum, tight crisp beat, procession". Mix: similar to parai but lighter; 150 Hz to 3 kHz. Same cultural care as parai.

### Urumi
- Build: double-headed hourglass-ish friction drum; a cord or rod rubbed through the skin makes growling, moaning sounds (F). Believed to have sacred powers; beats may induce trance (F).
- Context: ritual and temple of village deities, Amman festivals; an urumi melam (with nadaswaram, pambai) is associated with funerals and inauspicious occasions (F). Emotion: eerie, growling, ritual power.
- Prompt: "urumi drum, low growling friction, ritual, haunting". Mix: HPF 50 Hz, body 80-200 Hz, slow roll; slight low-pass; wide reverb. Cultural note: a death-rite sound; do not use as decor.

### Udukkai (udukku)
- Build: small hourglass drum (like damaru, idakka); squeezing the lacing in the middle bends pitch; played with the bare hand (F).
- Context: Mari Amman and village-deity temples; villupattu and ritual storytelling (F). Sound: high, snappy, with a pitch bend; Emotion: ritual, invocation, suspense.
- Prompt: "udukkai hourglass drum, snapping pitch bends, village temple ritual". Mix: HPF 200 Hz, 1-4 kHz, centre; dry room.

### Pambai
- Build: pair of cylindrical double-headed drums (F). Context: temple festivals, Murugan worship dance, naiyandi melam, urumi melam (F). Sound: deep, rolling, round; Emotion: festival pulse, ritual.
- Prompt: "pambai drums, deep paired cylinder drums, folk festival pulse". Mix: low-mid 100-250 Hz, wide pair; low reverb.

## D. Pairings (tradition, E)
- Periya melam: nadaswaram pair + ottu (drone) + thavil pair + talam cymbals (F).
- Urumi melam: nadaswaram + pambai + urumi (funerals, inauspicious) (F). Naiyandi melam: urumi or pambai-led folk/ritual dance (F).
- Concert: voice + violin + mridangam + ghatam or kanjira + morsing + tambura.
- Folk dance: parai + thappu + (nadaswaram or kombu) + clapping.
- Film hybrid: synth bed + Western strings + nadaswaram hook (see `western-instruments-deep.md` section 7).

## E. Cultural sensitivity (rules)
- Match instrument to context: parai at funerals and folk is not interchangeable with auspicious nadaswaram at a wedding. Do not put a death-rite instrument (urumi melam, oppari) into a joyful product ad without a reviewer.
- Oppari (mourning laments) are sung by women, near-unaccompanied or with parai and aerophones; they have been shaped by caste associations (F). Do not use oppari as background colour, and never synthesise a lament voice.
- Parai, thappu, urumi: link to Dalit heritage and pride; engage native consultants; credit players.
- Sacred instruments (temple and ritual): follow brand and religion guidelines; no trivial or comedic use.
- Never use a real community's ritual recording as a sample without permission.
