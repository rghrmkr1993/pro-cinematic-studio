# Theory and tune

Labels: F fact (standard music theory), E estimate or craft rule of thumb, U unverified. Use this to write precise briefs and prompts, and to talk to musicians.

## 1. Melody
- Melody = pitches plus rhythm over time. A memorable tune usually has a small range (about an octave or a ninth), mostly stepwise motion, one or two leaps, repetition with variation (E).
- Contour: arch (rise then fall), wave, ascending, descending. A leap up is often followed by a step back down (E).
- Phrase: 2 or 4 bars; question phrase then answer phrase. In AI prompts say "call and response phrases" for singable lines.
- Tension notes: leading tone, flat 2, tritone. Rest notes: tonic, fifth, third.
- Singable: stay within the vocal range of the voice type (typical comfortable solo range about 1.5 octaves, E); avoid fast leaps on long syllables.

## 2. Harmony and chords
- Triads: major (bright, stable), minor (sad, serious), diminished (unstable), augmented (dreamy, suspended). Sevenths add colour: maj7 (lush), min7 (soft, jazzy), dom7 (bluesy, wants to resolve).
- Suspended (sus2, sus4): open, unresolved, cinematic. Add9: modern pop shimmer. Pedal tone (held bass note): drones, suspense; matches the Indian tambura idea.
- Functions: tonic (home), subdominant (away), dominant (tension, wants home). Cadences: authentic V to I (final), plagal IV to I (amen, gentle), deceptive V to vi (surprise), half cadence ending on V (open).
- Roman numerals in a key: I ii iii IV V vi vii. In C major: C Dm Em F G Am Bdim.

## 3. Common progressions (F, standard; all are generic building blocks, not owned)
| Progression | Feel | Use |
|---|---|---|
| I - V - vi - IV | uplifting, familiar | pop, ads, hopeful |
| vi - IV - I - V | emotional, anthemic | ballad, build |
| I - vi - IV - V | nostalgic, 1950s | retro |
| ii - V - I | smooth resolution | jazz, soft pop |
| i - VII - VI - VII | driving, epic | action, trailers, rock |
| i - VI - III - VII | cinematic minor | drama, sci-fi |
| i - iv - V (or v) | serious, folk minor | thriller, ballad |
| 12-bar blues I I I I IV IV I I V IV I I | earthy | blues, rock and roll |
| I - bVII - IV | open, modal | folk rock, Mixolydian |
| Modal vamp (one or two chords) | hypnotic | ambient, ragas, trance |
Indian note: Indian classical is melody over a drone, not chord changes. Film songs add chords on top; keep chords simple and let the raga lead.

## 5. Rhythm and meter
- Beat = pulse; tempo in BPM; bar = grouping. 4/4 standard; 3/4 waltz (graceful); 6/8 (rolling, lilting, two groups of three); 5/4 and 7/8 (restless); 12/8 (slow blues, gospel).
- Syncopation = accents off the beat (funk, hip-hop, Afrobeats). Swing = long-short eighths (jazz). Straight = even. Half-time feel = backbeat on beat 3 (trap, heavy).
- Subdivision: 8ths, 16ths, triplets. Hi-hat at 16ths = busy, urban. Triplet hi-hat = trap.
- Indian rhythm: tala cycles (adi tala 8 beats, 4+2+2; rupaka 3; misra chapu 7; khanda chapu 5). Tamil film songs often sit on 4/4 with folk-drum feel. See the Indian reference.
- Polyrhythm: 3 against 2 (West African roots; Afrobeats, jazz).
- Always give the AI a BPM and a feel word: "108 BPM, straight, laid-back".

| Dance pop, K-pop | 100-130 |

| Rock, punk | 120-180 |

| Comedy, playful | 110-140 |

Maths: bars x 4 beats x 60 / BPM = seconds. At 120 BPM one 4/4 bar = 2 s. At 100 BPM one bar = 2.4 s. Choose BPM so key hits land on bar lines.

## 7. Song forms
- Verse-chorus: intro, verse, pre-chorus, chorus, verse, chorus, bridge, final chorus, outro. Verse tells; chorus is the hook; bridge contrasts (new chords or key).
- AABA (32 bar): two similar sections, a contrasting bridge, return (jazz standards).
- Strophic: same tune per verse (folk, hymns). Through-composed: always new (film cues).
- Tamil and Carnatic film song form (F, common): pallavi (main theme, the hook), anupallavi (second part, usually higher), charanam (verses, return to the pallavi after each). Film songs add interludes (BGM) between charanams, often a flute or strings line.
- Carnatic kriti: pallavi, anupallavi, charanam; also varnam (practice piece), thillana (rhythmic closer, often used for dance).
- Hindustani: sthayi (like pallavi) and antara (like anupallavi), then improvisation (bandish structure).
- Ad structure: hook in the first 2 s, develop, product beat, sting or button ending.
- Instrumental cue: motif, development, climax, resolution.

## 8. Hooks, motifs and leitmotifs
- Hook = the short, repeatable idea listeners remember (rhythmic, melodic or sonic). Make it 3 to 8 notes, repeated in the first 10 s, easy to hum, E.
- Motif = a small cell (2 to 5 notes) developed by sequence, inversion, augmentation, diminution, reharmonisation, instrument change, tempo change.
- Leitmotif = a motif tied to a character, place or idea; appears in different guises as the story changes (minor for loss, major for triumph, solo flute when alone, full orchestra when united). Details: `film-scoring.md`.
- Sonic hook: a unique sound (a plucked string, a bell, a vocal chop) used as brand sound.
- Earworm test: hum it from memory the next day.

## 9. Key and mode picks for briefs (E)
Hopeful: major or Lydian. Warm nostalgic: major with add9 or Mixolydian. Sad: minor, Dorian for bittersweet. Tense: Phrygian or chromatic, pedal tone. Epic: minor with big fifths and octave strings. Dream: Lydian or whole tone, reverb. Devotional: raga plus drone. Choose a key a singer can handle (A to E for most voices).

## 10. Words that help a musician or model
"stepwise, small range, repeating motif", "sparse arrangement, space between phrases", "pedal tone under", "modal, no strong cadences", "ends on the tonic, clean finish", "build every 4 bars, add one layer each time", "half-time feel", "lilting 6/8".
