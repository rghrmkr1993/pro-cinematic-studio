# Indian and Tamil music

Labels: F sourced (see sources.md), E general knowledge, U unverified. Mood labels for ragas are traditional and subjective; sources differ. Styles are described as techniques only. In prompts and briefs never name a composer, singer or a specific song; describe the era sound. A raga is a scale-and-phrase framework and is not owned; a composed tune is. Always get a Carnatic or Hindustani reviewer for hero cues.

## 1. Carnatic essentials
- Raga: a melodic framework with an ascent (arohanam) and descent (avarohanam), characteristic phrases, key notes and ornaments (gamakas). Gamakas are central; the bare scale gives only a rough picture (F).
- Swaras: Sa Ri Ga Ma Pa Da Ni. Sa and Pa are fixed; Ri, Ga, Ma, Da, Ni have variants. Tonic (Sa) is set by the drone (tambura or shruti box).
- Tala: rhythmic cycle. Adi (8 beats, 4+2+2) is most common; rupaka (3), misra chapu (7), khanda chapu (5), tisra and chatusra gati subdivisions. Film songs often simplify to 4/4 or 6/8.
- Forms: varnam, kriti (pallavi, anupallavi, charanam), tillana, alapana (free improvisation), niraval, kalpanaswaram, thani avartanam (percussion solo).
- Concert order (E): varnam first, then kritis, main piece (RTP), light pieces last.

## 2. The 72 melakarta concept (F)
- A melakarta is a parent scale with all seven notes in order, same up and down (sampurna). Each parent (janaka) gives derived ragas (janya) that may skip notes (audava 5, shadava 6) or zigzag (vakra).
- 72 are organised in 12 chakras of 6. Melas 1-36 use the lower Ma (Suddha Madhyama); 37-72 use the higher Ma (Prati Madhyama). Within a chakra only Da and Ni change. The katapayadi sutra maps raga names to numbers. Codified in the 17th century by Venkatamakhin; today's naming follows Govindacharya's pattern (F).
- Useful anchors: Sankarabharanam (29) = Western major. Kharaharapriya (22) = Dorian-like. Natabhairavi (20) = natural minor. Harikambhoji (28) = Mixolydian. Mechakalyani (65) = Lydian.

## 4. Hindustani basics (F where sourced)
- Thaats: ten parent scales (Bilawal, Kalyan, Khamaj, Bhairav, Poorvi, Marwa, Kafi, Asavari, Bhairavi, Todi), Bhatkhande system; an imperfect but useful classification (F).
- Raga time theory: each raga belongs to a time of day, in 3-hour prahars; Bhairav morning, Todi late morning, Bhimpalasi early afternoon, Madhuvanti late afternoon to evening, Bhoopali early night, Malkauns late night (F). Use for ambient stories ("dawn raga").
- Tala: teental 16 counts (4+4+4+4); theka is the drum pattern of a tala; jhaptal 10, ektal 12, rupak 7, dadra 6, keherwa 8.
- Structure: alap (free, slow), jor and jhala (pulse, speeding up), bandish (composition: sthayi and antara) with tabla; tarana, thumri (light classical, romantic), ghazal (poetry song), khayal (main vocal form), dhrupad (oldest, austere).
- Rasa: ragas linked to nine rasas (sringara love, karuna pathos, veera heroic, shanta peace, and others); Madhuvanti is a love raga (F).
- Cousins: Bhoopali ~ Mohanam; Pilu ~ Kapi; Malkauns ~ Hindolam (E).

## 5. Tamil folk and regional forms (E unless noted)
- **Therukoothu**: street theatre, sung and acted narrative; harmonium, mridangam, mugavina (reed), dholak, loud declamatory singing, make-up and costume; mythic stories.
- **Gaana**: Chennai working-class urban folk; street and drum rhythm, witty or emotional lyrics on daily life and loss; call and response, thappu or parai, gaana vocals; entered mainstream film music in recent decades and is used for social commentary (F, Esplanade).
- **Oppari**: mourning lament, sung by women, unaccompanied or with light drum; raw, plaintive. Handle with respect; do not use as colour.
- **Villupattu**: bow-song storytelling; large bow (vil) struck with sticks, udukku, ghatam, kudam; chorus repeats; mythic and local legends.
- **Kuthu**: fast dance beat, parai, thappu and electronic drums, 100-140 BPM; energetic, celebratory, crowd chants.
- **Naattupura paattu**: village or rural folk songs; field work, festivals, simple tunes, hand drums, flute, nadaswaram, clap and call and response. Pentatonic and modal tunes.
- **Karakattam, kavadi attam, thappattam, silambattam**: dance and martial folk with parai, thavil, urumi.
- **Kolattam and kummi**: rhythmic stick or clap dances.

## 6. Devotional
- **Bhajan**: simple repeated devotional song, harmonium, tabla, cymbals (manjira), group singing. **Kirtan**: call-and-response chanting, builds in speed and intensity. **Thevaram**: Tamil Shaiva hymns of the Nayanmars, set in pann (ancient modes) with ritual temple singing, often with oduvar (singers), nadaswaram and thavil in temples (F-level history, E style). **Tiruppugazh**: Murugan hymns, intricate rhythms. **Divya Prabandham**: Vaishnava hymns. **Suprabhatam**: dawn awakening of a deity (E: Mayamalavagowla-like morning feel, bells, soft).
- Production: warm reverb, drone, bells (see sound-design), choir doubles, avoid slick EDM unless the brief asks.

## 7. Tamil film music eras (era sound only)
| Era | Sound (techniques) |
|---|---|
| 1950s-60s (E, supported by 3P) | raga-based melodies with Western-style orchestration; strings in unison with the voice, mandolin and clarinet obbligato, Latin and light-classical rhythm touches, tabla and mridangam with brushes, playback singing, romantic duets, lush and tuneful, mono to early stereo |
| 1970s-80s | orchestral-folk fusion: folk rhythm and folk tunes with counterpoint strings, layered flutes, string countermelodies, synthesisers and drum machines arriving, long through-composed interludes, richer harmony, dedicated background scores; multitrack recording |
| 1990s | synth and digital revolution: sampled and programmed percussion, layered synth pads, polished digital reverb, world instruments, ambient textures, crossover to pan-Indian audiences, studio-driven, high-fidelity |
| 2000s-2020s | broader palette: club, R&B, electronica, hip-hop and trap beats, indie guitar bands, gaana and folk samples back in, 808 and EDM drops, rap interludes, cinematic hybrid scores, regional-folk recoveries with vintage instruments |

## 8. Other film music traits (E)
- **Bollywood**: song-and-dance set pieces; qawwali and ghazal influences; dholak and tabla; Punjabi bhangra beats (dhol, tumbi); big choruses; item numbers; heavy Hindi-Urdu poetry; 90s melodic romance era; modern pop and EDM crossovers; multi-singer.
- **Malayalam**: strong light-classical roots, sensitive melodies, flute and strings, chenda in festival and folk songs, thoughtful lyrics, melodic restraint, rain and backwater moods; recent films use acoustic, indie and ambient textures.
- **Telugu**: large-scale mass songs, folk dance numbers, dappu and dhol beats, melodic duets, heavy orchestral hero themes.
- **Kannada**: melodic traditions, Hindustani and Carnatic blend, folk (Yakshagana drums and chande), rock-pop modern scene.
- Region specific tuning: native-reviewer check for pronunciation and vocal style in each language; Telugu and Malayalam voice and vocal quality in AI tools is U until tested.

## 9. Briefing a human Carnatic or folk musician
Provide: raga (or mood), tala, tempo, key (Sa pitch, for example C or D), reference described in words, form (pallavi, anupallavi, charanam), instrument, vocal range, lyrics owner, timing to picture, credit and fee. Ask them for alapana or an introduction length. Record the tambura and shruti first. Do not ask for "like [song]".

## 10. Prompt wording for AI
"Carnatic-inspired instrumental, Hindolam-like scale (Sa Ga Ma Da Ni), bamboo flute lead, tambura drone, mridangam adi-tala pattern, 70 BPM, devotional calm." Test output by ear; if the scale is off, state the notes. Tamil vocals via ElevenLabs Music are U; test one take (ai-music-prompting.md).
