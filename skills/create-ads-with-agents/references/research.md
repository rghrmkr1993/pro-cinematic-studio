# Research protocol

Apply the mode and stage rules in [workflow](workflow.md) first. This reference describes live research; a demo reuses supplied material or labeled illustrative assumptions and does not browse unless explicitly requested. Complete and show the audience synthesis before beginning competitor-ad analysis.

Use current web sources for market, product, price and advertising facts in live mode. Record retrieval date, region, page title, URL or supplied file location, source owner, evidence type and access limitations. Cite the precise supporting page. Follow host browsing and quotation rules.

## Evidence registry

For each material claim record: ID; exact proposition; source IDs; scope (SKU/category/market/time); status (observed, user-provided, inferred, proposed or unsupported); confidence rationale; contradictions; permissible ad wording.

Preserve original observations and source/ad IDs. Record later corrections, their basis and affected conclusions separately; do not silently rewrite earlier evidence to fit a preferred concept.

A seller page supports what the seller states, not independent efficacy or sales. A retailer review count does not equal purchases in the target market. Syndicated reviews and repeated press releases are not independent sources. Rankings and search trends are demand signals, not unit sales. Distinguish a current observation from a historical report.

## Buyer research

Begin with the offer and the alternatives people actually use, including doing nothing. Identify what changes at the moment of purchase and what progress the person seeks. Build segments around materially different situations, outcomes or barriers; do not replace those with age/gender personas or invent a fixed number of segments.

Maintain a compact matrix: source/coverage/date → buyer language or faithful paraphrase → situation/trigger → desired outcome → current alternative → hesitation → offer fit/poor fit → needed proof → creative implication. Mark direct observations, interpretations and hypotheses separately. Quoted words are exact excerpts, translations are labeled, and search snippets do not count as fully inspected reviews.


Combine primary product specifications, retailer reviews and independent user discussion when available. Seek actual situations: what happened, what was tried, why the person paid, what worked, what disappointed, and why they returned or repurchased. Check review recency, incentives and variant identity. Do not treat a "verified purchase" label as a guarantee that the review is authentic.

For a deep run, aim for roughly 20 useful review observations across at least two source types per finalist. This is a coverage target, not permission to invent a quota or imply statistical representativeness. Narrow or broaden according to available evidence and the user's scope. Report the actual count; do not count snippets as fully read reviews.

Return a buyer-language table: situation, desired outcome, objection, supporting observations, counterexample, creative implication. Before ad analysis or generation preparation, show the buyer groups and recommended priority directly in chat, including alternatives, poor-fit situations and evidence gaps. Record the current audience decision according to state.md; do not present hooks as audience findings. Use short compliant quotations only when wording matters; otherwise paraphrase.

## Meta Ad Library

After the audience synthesis, attempt Meta Ad Library analysis for each live new ad strategy or production brief, unless the user restricts research or the current run already contains adequate inspected Library evidence. Demo mode does not require an external Library visit. Reuse that evidence with its date and coverage. Search public sources and available read-only connectors first according to host rules. If these fail, try the authorized browser route when available; follow the browser skill. An unsuccessful web fetch alone does not exhaust an available browser. Do not bypass access controls. If accessible, set country and appropriate category, search actual advertisers, and open ad details and destination URLs. Record:

- Advertiser/page identity, ad ID and direct reference.
- Selected country, observed status and dates, inspection date.
- Format, opening hook, visible use case, demonstration/proof, offer, CTA.
- Whether the creative is a variant or a genuinely different concept.
- Buyer motivation, concept family, proof approach, visual progression and placement cues; distinguish observed execution from inferred persuasion. Use meta-andromeda.md when interpreting Meta creative diversity.
- Destination and observed funnel steps.
- Actual content coverage: full video/audio, sampled frames, still/copy only, or unavailable.

For deep research, seek at least three relevant advertisers and approximately 20 distinct ad observations when accessible. State actual coverage and duplication. Do not equate cards, variants, campaigns or advertisers.

A long-running ad, repeated angle or localized versions suggests a hypothesis worth testing. Inspect such families early and record the shared mechanism, differences, markets, languages, dates and relevant destination. Cluster repeated and translated versions as one family rather than independent confirmations. Recurrence can reflect spend, automation or inconclusive tests; it does not reveal conversions, profitability, budget, audience targeting or exact campaign setup. Adapt useful communication mechanics into an original execution, without copying distinctive expression, brands, characters or customer evidence. Public EU disclosures or political-ad data have different scopes; never generalize them to all US commercial ads.

For algorithm-related claims, record the primary Meta source, publication/retrieval dates and inspected coverage, then label engineering fact, advertiser guidance or team inference. A workshop listing is not the full lesson or study. Do not treat ad-library prevalence as evidence of a private Andromeda preference.

If access fails, retain the exact failure and supplied screenshots/exports or accessible sources. Call the result limited competitor research, not a completed Library audit. Do not bypass login, CAPTCHA, permissions or paywalls.

## Funnel inspection

Separate observed competitor flow from the recommended flow. Inspect reachable steps: ad promise → destination → proof → offer → objections → CTA → checkout or lead form. Record prices, shipping, subscription conditions and guarantees as of the visit. Do not buy, submit real contact data or contact sales to complete inspection.

A quiz may be explored with clearly hypothetical, non-sensitive selections when allowed. Stop before submission that creates a lead, account, order or other external side effect. Do not invent hidden post-purchase steps. Match the proposed flow to intent: direct purchase, consultation, trial, demo or course enrollment as appropriate.

## Discovery

Begin broadly enough to avoid anchoring on familiar categories. Use the user's constraints; absent constraints, distinguish low-friction physical products, recurring consumables, digital offers and services rather than pretending one scoring formula fits all.

Compare candidates on:
- Evidence of current demand and actual spending.
- Urgency/use frequency and dissatisfaction with alternatives.
- Clear, truthful demonstration in a short ad.
- Differentiation and crowdedness.
- Price relative to known costs, returns, fulfillment and repeat buying.
- Operational and claims constraints material to this specific offer.

Use explicit observations and uncertainty instead of invented precision scores. Compute contribution or break-even acquisition cost only with supplied or sourced inputs, labeling assumptions. Do not treat category TAM as an obtainable market or project LTV without retention evidence.

The final recommendation explains why the selected options deserve testing and what evidence could overturn that choice. The creative/funnel recommendation must use the same product, market and price that were researched.
