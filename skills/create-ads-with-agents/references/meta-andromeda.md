Keep three evidence classes visible in research and briefs:

1. **Meta engineering fact** — what a named primary source actually discloses about retrieval or ranking.
2. **Meta creative guidance** — advertiser-facing guidance, with placement and study scope. Do not relabel it as Andromeda internals.
3. **Team hypothesis** — the proposed audience situation, persuasive mechanism, scene design and experiment. Explain the inference and how to test it.

The workflow below is the team's practical synthesis. It is not Meta's scoring system. Treat Andromeda as one part of delivery, not the whole auction, ranking stack or organic Reels algorithm. Creative meaning can inform a relevance hypothesis; it cannot guarantee which people receive an ad. Do not claim that putting a job title, object or keyword in a video mechanically targets that audience.

Exclude unsupported recipes: hidden similarity percentages, exact algorithm feature weights, universal ad-count requirements, guaranteed reach from many uploads, fixed optimal durations, and compulsory UGC or rapid cuts. Never transfer Meta's aggregate lift figures to a user's expected CPA/ROAS. Campaign eligibility, objective, offer, landing experience, measurement, budget and actual response still matter. Verify campaign-specific requirements only when that stage is in scope.

## Build a useful concept portfolio

1. **Start with a purchase situation.** Use the buyer evidence to identify a trigger, desired outcome, objection and available proof. For a supplied product, skip niche discovery. For a discovery task, apply this framework to the selected candidates. Do not default to a particular niche or force every offer into a lifestyle montage.
2. **Map distinct reasons to act.** Explore plausible motivation/proof combinations internally, then return the requested number. For a full e-commerce portfolio use Callout Diagram, Review Card and Offer Card with PROOF; for services use QUEST as defined in marketing-frameworks.md. These are this workflow's requirements, not Meta's. Respect narrower explicit scope. Missing proof stays an explicitly unfinished blueprint or requires an agreed substitution; do not fabricate it to fill the set.
3. **Assign families and variants.** Give each strategic concept a `concept_family_id`. Record its buyer situation, main motivation, promise, proof approach and visual story. Label a new opening, music choice, caption, aspect ratio or actor as a variant when the underlying persuasive idea stays the same. A format change can be worth testing without becoming a new buyer motivation. Never claim these labels reproduce Meta's internal classification.
4. **Diversify both meaning and execution.** Across the selected set, explain the substantive difference and make it visible through an appropriate scene, demonstration, artifact, presenter or narrative structure. Avoid a batch with different captions over the same body. Preserve product identity and brand coherence. If only hook variants were requested, supply variants and label them honestly; do not force extra concepts.
5. **Choose what deserves production first.** Compare strength of buyer evidence, clarity of the purchase reason, credibility of proof, visible distinctiveness and feasible execution within the actual budget. Name the strongest reason and biggest risk for each. Use a reasoned priority, not an invented winning probability, simulated ROAS or numerical "Andromeda score."

Use this comparison before writing full scenes:

| Concept family / variant | Buyer situation and motivation | Promise and proof available | Opening and visual story | Meaningful difference from the others | Business event / main risk / test priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Adapt the proof and destination to the business. Physical goods can show actual use, construction or a verified comparison. A service can show its process, a real deliverable or a documented case. A course can show a real lesson and relevant learning task. An app can show authentic UI and the activation action. For B2B, show the particular buyer's workflow and qualifying use case. Synthetic images illustrate a concept; they are not proof of capacity, durability, efficacy or client results.

## Required Meta creative brief

For each selected concept, produce a compact, executable brief. Scale the detail to the requested deliverable; a short idea request needs a short card, not a full production dossier.

| Field | Required decision |
| --- | --- |
| Goal and placement | Identify the business event, market, audience language and intended placement. Keep campaign targeting and optimization assumptions separate from the creative. |
| Situation and motivation | State who recognizes the situation, what they want and the evidence supporting that interpretation. Avoid invented or sensitive personal assumptions. |
| Family and difference | Record family/variant IDs and the specific persuasion or execution difference being tested. |
| Promise and price | State one main reason to consider this offer and only confirmed terms. Give a relevant qualification cue when it helps the buyer decide. |
| Opening | Describe the exact first image/action and spoken/on-screen copy. Explain why it earns attention from a plausible buyer and connects to the offer. A distracting spectacle alone is insufficient. |
| Visible proof | Identify the actual action, detail, UI, deliverable or documented result that supports the promise, its source and its reveal point. Mark illustration separately from proof and name missing assets. |
| Progression and payoff | Map timed beats: what new information/action each adds, when the opening pays off and what moves the viewer toward the next step. Justify duration from the task, rather than filling a model's clip length. |
| Product and brand | Specify the identity anchors, product scale, setting/outfit cues and moments when the product or service becomes clear. Do not let a presenter or decorative setting obscure the offer. |
| Placement execution | For Reels, plan vertical framing, purposeful audio and safe-zone composition using current specifications (M4). Make core meaning understandable muted; check copy at phone size. For Feed/Stories, choose and verify the intended placement's current crop and interface constraints. Do not hardcode one safe-area template for every placement. |
| CTA and destination | Provide one clear next action and the matching landing headline, evidence, terms and action. State the observed destination or the proposed flow. |
| Production handoff | Supply scene IDs, reference IDs, shot framing/action/camera, continuity anchors, audio/text, generated versus supplied media, and scene-specific acceptance checks. Keep model-ready prompts faithful to this brief; discover current provider capabilities before submitting. |
| Why test / how to judge | Connect the observed buyer evidence to a plausible response, label platform facts versus hypotheses, name a primary business metric and diagnostic metrics, and state the finding that would weaken this idea. |

Treat checking the opening seconds as an editorial heuristic, not a disclosed algorithm threshold. Start with a clear product, recognisable situation, useful question or credible outcome; choose what fits this concept. Use pacing that makes actions and proof readable. Neither a slow fashion shot nor a longer explanation is automatically wrong when it serves the message.

## Assign work to the team

Reuse the existing specialists; a Meta lens does not require extra always-running agents.

- Buyer researcher: return purchase situations and motivations with evidence, plus the proof people need before acting.
- Ad/funnel analyst: classify observed ads by concept family, proof approach and execution; identify coverage gaps. Call repeated public approaches hypotheses, not profitable ads or known algorithm preferences.
- Strategist: own the concept portfolio, the Meta brief and the explicit fact/guidance/hypothesis distinction. The producer can perform this role.
- Director: make the difference between concepts visible, write the precise beats and define perceptual acceptance criteria before production.
- Independent reviewer: check the underlying idea, current draft and actual output. Return `pass`, `revise` or `unverified` with a specific reason; never infer a conversion win from aesthetics.
- Experiment analyst, when results exist: relate creative families to properly measured business outcomes and specify the next informative comparison.

## Review creative execution

Review this separately from file validity and artifact detection. Give independent verdicts for factual support, creative execution, technical/media coverage and test readiness. A successful render or a clean frame does not make the creative persuasive.

- **Comprehension:** Can a viewer identify the offer, situation and benefit? If a concept promises different settings or use cases, are those distinctions visible in the frames themselves?
- **Progression:** Does each beat add evidence, a completed action, emotion or a useful decision cue? Flag prolonged repetition, empty pauses, redundant teaser/body footage and an ending that breaks the story. Do not demand cuts merely to hit an arbitrary pace.
- **Product priority:** Do composition, detail and human interaction make the actual product/service desirable and understandable? Evaluate according to the chosen brand treatment, not a universal requirement for glossy or lo-fi footage.
- **Proof and payoff:** Is the promised demonstration actually completed and legible? A pile of props beside a product does not show those props fit inside it. A location label does not establish that the setting reads correctly.
- **Portfolio difference:** Compare the whole scripts/edits, not only hooks and filenames. Record shared-body variants as variants, while preserving useful controlled tests.
- **Placement and finish:** Check crop, readable copy, sound design/speech, brand consistency, CTA and destination match within actual inspection coverage.

For a claimed visual benefit, review the relevant frames without explanatory scene labels; retain necessary text for inherently textual offers. For the final edit, review continuous video/audio when supported. Sampled frames can support a composition verdict, but not certify rhythm, motion or sound. Report those modalities as unverified and do not issue an unconditional final creative pass. When the user rejects the visual result, reopen the creative gate and investigate the actual output; do not let an earlier technical pass overrule their brief or feedback. Repairs still follow the existing authorization, budget and retry cap.

## Test and learn

Separate **concept exploration** from **controlled refinement**. First learn which substantive approaches deserve further investment; then isolate an opening, proof order, format or CTA within a family. State what is held constant and what changes. Respect a user who requested only one creative.

For a sales objective, evaluate purchases and acquisition cost against known contribution economics. For services/B2B, use qualified leads, attended bookings or signed business as appropriate; cheap leads alone may be misleading. For courses/apps, use the actual paid or activation outcome. For an awareness objective, choose a suitable brand/reach outcome rather than forcing a purchase metric.

Define the measurement window, attribution, spend and denominators. Use the actual available metric definitions for opening retention, video watch behavior, outbound clicks and landing conversion. Treat these as diagnostics, not proof of revenue or proprietary ranking weights. Choose only the metrics needed for the hypothesis; do not invent universal benchmarks, minimum spend, 72-hour rules or a guaranteed sample size.

Automated delivery may allocate unequal exposure. An ad receiving more spend is not a randomized winner, and low delivery is not evidence of creative failure. Distinguish exploratory results from a controlled experiment. Before ranking creatives, inspect the supplied measurement definitions and outcome quality. Describe a useful controlled comparison when a causal answer is needed; campaign configuration, tracking setup, activation and monitoring remain outside this creative skill.

Feed observed performance back into the motivation/proof/format matrix. Keep the best-supported mechanism, repair the named bottleneck and retire a weak hypothesis only when the evidence permits. Do not respond to every weak result with more generated variants.

## Deliver the recommendation

For each idea, answer simply: **who this speaks to; why they might care; what the viewer sees as support; how this differs; why it deserves a test; what result would change the decision.** Include generation-ready scenes when requested. Attribute platform claims to the appropriate primary source; present creative decisions as reasoned proposals. The outcome is a reviewed creative hypothesis, not an algorithm-certified winner.
