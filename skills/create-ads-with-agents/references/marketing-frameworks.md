# Marketing frameworks and required portfolios

Use the user's actual product, offer and market. Frameworks organize persuasion; they do not authorize invented evidence or a larger assignment.

## Required marketing decisions

Before copy, specify:
- The purchase situation and job the buyer wants to complete.
- The desired outcome and relevant obstacle, based on buyer evidence.
- The product/service mechanism and the one supported difference that matters here.
- The main objection and the evidence or truthful explanation that answers it.
- The real offer: price, inclusions, conditions, destination and next action.
- The measurable hypothesis: what will change, for whom, and what observed result would support or reject it.

Choose specificity over generic claims such as “unlock your potential,” “game-changing,” “premium quality,” or “effortless transformation.” A feature earns space by explaining a relevant benefit. Do not equate a low price with poor quality or invent a luxury-price comparison. Do not manufacture emotional distress or make assumptions about a viewer's sensitive personal attributes.

## E-commerce: PROOF

Use the production brief's **Promise → Receipts → Offer → Objection → First step**:
- Promise: one plausible buyer outcome.
- Receipts: actual product details, authentic customer evidence or a substantiated demonstration.
- Offer: the real product, price and applicable terms.
- Objection: answer the most relevant hesitation with supported information.
- First step: one explicit CTA that the destination fulfills.

PROOF is a planning checklist, not a demand for five text blocks in every ad. A short static can express several parts through imagery and the adjoining caption/landing page; do not hide material terms or the CTA.

## The three required e-commerce ad types

A full three-concept or full-portfolio e-commerce brief includes these three types. An explicit request for one ad, a single format or another count controls scope; use this trio as the portfolio's foundation rather than tripling every request.

| Type | Static execution | Video counterpart | Evidence requirement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Callout Diagram | Product hero with a few precise leaders pointing to actual features; connect each to a relevant benefit | Product overview or demonstration with meaningful action and timed callouts | Verified visible features and supported benefits; do not invent internal compartments or hidden functions |
| Review Card | A readable genuine customer quote with accurately attributed, authorized identity/rating when available | Authentic review-led UGC, with source, consent and accurate representation | Traceable genuine review/testimonial; no invented stars, names, quotes or “verified buyer” labels |
| Offer Card | Product, real price/inclusions, material terms and one CTA | Unboxing or offer reveal showing what is actually included | Current price, contents and terms; discount/urgency/guarantee only if supported |

A format does not supply the buyer insight. Make the three concepts differ meaningfully in motivation, objection or proof approach. Never pad the set with three near-identical hooks.

### Missing review

If no usable customer review exists, label that part **Review Card blueprint — awaiting authentic review**. Supply the layout and the exact evidence needed; keep it out of final ad exports. Its buyer angle and test priority remain provisional until the actual review supports them. Continue the other concepts.

Do not put placeholder quotations, synthetic ratings or fictional customer identities in a ready-to-run ad. If the user needs three finished creatives, obtain an approved review or the user's permission to substitute a product-proof card/actor-led demonstration. Label that substitution accurately; it does not become a Review Card. A generated presenter can explain a product without claiming personal purchase, use or results.

### Unboxing limits

Match the actual packaging, included accessories and item count. Without packaging evidence, use an honest product/offer reveal; do not invent a gift box, warranty card or extra accessory. A photorealistic packing demonstration must not imply unverified capacity or airline compliance.

## Services: QUEST

Use **Qualify → Understand → Educate → Stimulate → Transition**, adapted from [Michel Fortin's QUEST formula](https://michelfortin.com/quest-formula/).

Identify the relevant situation, show that the problem is understood, explain the actual service mechanism, make its supported value concrete, and transition to the next step. Stimulate interest through useful specifics, not fear, shame or invented urgency.

For a three-concept service brief, use the production brief's complementary routes:
1. **Quiz:** a useful self-assessment leading to the real next step. Do not claim a diagnosis, guaranteed eligibility or nonexistent personalized output.
2. **Case study story:** documented starting situation, actual intervention and supported result, with context and permission. Without case evidence, prepare a blueprint or a clearly labeled process walkthrough.
3. **Direct offer:** precise scope, inclusions, terms and CTA. Include a guarantee only when the business actually offers it and its conditions are supplied. A guarantee is not mandatory.

QUEST may be expressed across the video, caption and destination. The visible ad must remain understandable on its own. Service geography, appointment availability and eligibility must be accurate.

## Notes, boards and native-looking static treatments

Use a handwritten note, legal pad, checklist, whiteboard, sidewalk board or simple message-style layout when it makes the pitch credible and easy to scan. These are treatments of an appropriate ad type, not evidence and not a fourth mandatory portfolio category.

The commercial hierarchy remains: situation/hook → useful details or supported proof → offer → CTA. Keep letterforms intentional and readable. A legal-pad treatment can remain plain; do not decorate it into a glossy interface. Avoid fake private conversations, fabricated customer messages, official notices, platform alerts or false interface affordances.

Adapt the supplied examples at the level of communication mechanics: a large readable heading, short useful list, clear benefit and next step. Replace third-party brands, phone numbers, art, mascots and specific copy with original authorized content. Do not import their claims.

## Testing and localization

Describe each concept as a hypothesis until actual campaign data supports a result. Choose the primary metric from the business goal (purchase, qualified lead, booked appointment, trial), and use attention/CTR metrics diagnostically. When available, assess downstream quality, acquisition cost and contribution with explicit inputs.

Recurring or translated competitor ads deserve inspection and may inspire the next test. They may also reflect automation, low spend or an inconclusive experiment. Cluster related creatives as one concept family; do not count each translation as independent confirmation.

Localize buyer context, spelling, units, currency, offer eligibility, cultural meaning, voice timing and line breaks. A translation of a winning ad is not automatically a winner in another market. Recheck claims, typography, pacing and destination for every market.
