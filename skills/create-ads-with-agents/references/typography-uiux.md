# Typography and UI/UX for advertising

Design for the actual placement and a distracted viewer on a phone. Make the offer and next step easy to understand before adding visual style. A clean layout does not substantiate a claim.

## Hierarchy and composition

Use one dominant message, one visual proof focus and one primary CTA. Establish a clear reading order through scale, contrast, proximity and whitespace. Group related price/terms; align edges and repeat spacing consistently. Keep callout lines short, unambiguous and attached to the actual referenced product detail. Never draw a leader toward an invented feature.

Choose the crop before placing copy. Leave appropriate negative space without shrinking the product into a background decoration. Recompose for each requested ratio instead of stretching or blindly cropping a single master. Check current placement overlays and safe areas in the actual preview; a remembered universal pixel margin is not a substitute.

Show the ad at expected phone size. The main message and CTA should be readable without zooming. If a useful detail disappears, shorten copy or simplify the hierarchy. Disclaimers must be readable and near the claim they qualify; they cannot rescue a misleading headline.

## Type and color

Use a contemporary, restrained type system suited to the brand: typically one family, two or three weights, and a small set of consistent sizes. Inter, Manrope and DM Sans are examples to consider, not mandatory dependencies; verify the actual font file's license before use or distribution. Preserve an established authorized brand system.

Use sentence case where helpful, deliberate line breaks, consistent punctuation and enough leading. Avoid excessive tracking, long all-caps passages, outline-only body text, random font mixtures, novelty 3D headlines and ornamental letterforms that slow reading. Display type can carry a short hook; supporting terms need simpler text.

Choose colors by hierarchy and material context, not an automatic neon gradient. Use W3C's [contrast guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html) as a legibility benchmark: 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text. This benchmark does not certify an ad or its landing page as WCAG compliant. Test text against its actual still or moving background and do not rely only on color to distinguish information.

For note/board ads, a clear handwritten face or authorized real handwriting may be appropriate. Keep natural paper, believable writing size, baseline variation and margins. Avoid copied signatures, handwriting impersonation, illegible faux handwriting and decorative dirt used as a shortcut for authenticity.

## Exact copy and controlled finishing

Maintain a copy manifest with exact headline, supporting copy, price/conditions, CTA, language and timing. Prefer generating the photographic layer and composing critical typography, logos and interface graphics in a controlled editing layer. This deterministic finishing is permitted; all newly generated image/video/audio content still uses the locked Higgsfield stack.

When the requested model renders physical writing or labels, inspect every letter, number and mark. OCR is a useful secondary check, not a replacement for visual inspection. Correct a local text error in its layer instead of regenerating the entire accepted product or person.

If exact text assembly is unavailable, deliver the clean visual and an explicit unfinished layout plan. Do not mark an asset final while the required CTA or terms are missing or garbled.

## UI truth and conversion continuity

Use genuine authorized screenshots for real product interfaces where accuracy matters. Label a conceptual interface as a demo. Do not fabricate customer dashboards, calendar bookings, financial balances, trust badges, app ratings, chat messages or result notifications.

A depicted CTA should have one clear action and correspond to the real click destination. Do not fake close controls, play buttons, notifications or system warnings to trick interaction. A quiz invitation must lead to a real quiz or accurately named information page; “Shop” should not unexpectedly become a consultation form.

Keep offer, price, product variant and relevant terms consistent across ad and destination. Reduce unnecessary choices and explain the next step. Do not use prechecked consent, disguised recurring fees, false countdowns or obstructive dismissal patterns.

## Video typography

Use concise captions synchronized to meaning, with readable line lengths and stable placement. Keep captions away from faces, demonstration details and platform controls. Motion may introduce hierarchy, but each phrase needs enough settled time to read at normal playback speed. Avoid continual bouncing, word flashing or constant zooms that obscure the proof.

Hold the final CTA long enough for its actual copy, and review it with platform overlays. Preserve message comprehension when muted; audio adds value rather than carrying the only offer or next step.

## Final visual checks

Inspect the exact exported file, not only the design canvas:
- The first glance communicates the buyer situation or offer.
- Product, face and evidence remain recognizable at placement size.
- Text matches the copy manifest; line breaks, symbols, prices and punctuation survive export.
- Contrast and safe areas remain sound through the full video.
- Callouts identify real features; the CTA matches the destination.
- No incidental third-party branding, fake UI, missing terms or unusable tiny text remains.
