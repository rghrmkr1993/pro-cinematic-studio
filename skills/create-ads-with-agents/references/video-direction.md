# Video direction: dynamic, credible advertising

This is a self-contained adaptation of the supplied CINEDANCE 2.5 material. It includes the required directing principles rather than depending on a missing base skill, workbench, acting system or auditor. It is a creative workflow, not a guarantee about model internals or timing accuracy.

## Route and actual capabilities

Generate every video through **Higgsfield using Seedance 2.5**. Never silently switch to another model, provider or older version. Query the connected route before preparing a submission and record its actual model ID, supported durations and dimensions, reference types/counts, audio controls, pricing and available editing/extension operations.

The supplied source's statements about 30-second passes, 1080p, 30/10/10 reference ceilings, precise cut timing, `@imageN` syntax, timestamp edits and extensions are not portable promises. Verify each needed capability against the current Higgsfield tool response or authoritative route documentation. Do not infer it from a model name, an example prompt or another provider. If a required capability is missing, adapt within verified Seedance 2.5 capabilities or leave the affected stage explicitly unsubmitted.

Set technical output parameters in supported tool fields. Prompt timestamps express intended choreography; inspect the returned media to determine actual timing. Never describe intended timestamps as measured output.

## Reference route versus simple text-to-video

Use references when the ad requires exact product identity, a recurring person, a specific location, a controlled physical interaction or continuity across shots. The dependency sequence is needed **location plates → characters in Seedream 5 → product/prop sheets in Nano Banana Pro → Seedance 2.5 video**, all through Higgsfield. Each approved output becomes the authority for downstream work. Skip stages with no visible job; a product-only ad does not need a character sheet.

Simple text-to-video is allowed when no exact product, person or location must be preserved, no customer/performance evidence is being simulated, and one straightforward scene can communicate the idea. Record why references would add no needed control. Example: a generic moving paper texture behind exact graphic copy. It must still meet the same realism, rights, CTA and review checks. Do not use text-to-video to approximate an advertised SKU whose appearance matters.

A generated demonstration is an illustration, not evidence that a real product performs as shown. Preserve actual supplied proof footage where the claim requires it. Do not hide missing proof inside photorealistic action, synthetic testimonials or reconstructed before/after footage.

## Direct useful change

Every video ad must be dynamic through purposeful change: an action, a reveal, a comparison, a new piece of evidence, an answered objection or a clear transition to the offer. Motion should make the message easier to understand. A moving camera alone does not rescue a static idea.

Give the opening a clear visual question or buyer situation. Show the relevant subject promptly. Connect the middle to an observable demonstration or truthful service mechanism. End with an explicit CTA and enough stable screen time to read it. Match that action to the real destination.

Do not impose a universal cut every one or two seconds. A continuous proof shot may be the strongest choice. Cuts must not conceal loading, change the product, skip the difficult part of a demonstration or imply a result the footage has not shown. Avoid meaningless whip pans, random speed ramps, perpetual zooms, unrelated stock-like cutaways and transitions that obscure the product.

## Build one controllable unit at a time

For each generation unit, state one sentence describing its job. Prefer one coherent setting and situation. Separate a location change, time jump or technically dense interaction when that improves control; use supported generation and assembly capabilities rather than assuming an extension feature exists.

Plan the first frame before motion. Identify who and what is visible, their screen positions, distance, orientation, contact points, room to move and the intended focus. If an approved image is a first-frame constraint, say so. A location-only reference does not also control the camera angle or frame unless assigned that role.

Check feasibility before spending: the action must fit the available time and space; the object can physically do what is asked; a camera move can exist in the room; the scene has sufficient references; the voiceover can be spoken naturally; and the product evidence is supported. Simplify the action or split the unit when necessary. Do not cure an impossible sequence by adding more adjectives.

## Timed stages, framing and end states

Create a compact stage table before compiling the prompt:

| Field | Required decision |
| --- | --- |
| Time range | Intended start and end within the supported unit duration |
| Commercial job | What useful information the viewer gains |
| Framing | Subject, shot scale, viewpoint and useful camera distance |
| Action | One dominant action described through cause and consequence |
| Camera | One main strategy: hold, follow, reveal or a simple motivated move |
| End state | Final positions, object state, visible evidence and preserved identity |
| Transition | Continuous action, an explicit cut or a planned assembly boundary |
| Copy/audio | Exact words, cue timing and the place they belong |

Use headings such as `0–4s — Shot 1 — Table-level medium view`. Timestamps lead for consistent human review; this is a production convention, not a claim about token weighting. Keep ranges contiguous and non-overlapping when they represent a continuous pass. Use time ranges consistently for cross-references. A simple single action does not need arbitrary subdivisions. Stage count follows scene complexity and verified route limits, not a purported universal stability threshold.

State what the frame contains at every stage's end. A later shot must begin from that state or mark the change clearly. Preserve screen direction across cuts unless a new establishing view makes the reversal understandable. Avoid unsupported numerical FOV or shutter settings; use camera terms only when they change a visible decision. See [image direction](image-direction.md) for geometry and lighting discipline.

## Cause, contact, consequence and acting tasks

Write physical events in observable order: approach → contact → force or weight transfer → consequence → recovery. For example: the hand grips the handle, the fingers close, the handle straightens under load, the object clears the surface, then the arm settles. Do not jump from an untouched prop to a completed action. Include the relevant contact or resistance when it is needed for credibility.

Keep the product's weight, shape, closures and function consistent with real evidence. Hands do not pass through straps, objects do not teleport between cuts, and liquids or fabric follow the action that moved them. Intentional surreal imagery must remain visibly distinct from factual proof.

Give actors practical tasks and a reason to perform them. “Check the label, find the matching item and place it beside the bag” is more directable than “be confident and authentic.” Specify the target of attention, the next action and an observable response. A small pause, glance or change of grip may communicate a decision without an exaggerated advertising smile.

For dialogue, distinguish the speaker from voiceover. Use short exact lines and plausible breathing room. Do not invent personal use, customer status, credentials or a testimonial for a generated presenter. Obtain the necessary rights for real people, voices and supplied performances.

## Scoped references and canonical continuity

Maintain a reference manifest with actual file IDs and one explicit role per file or coherent reference set: face/wardrobe, product identity, location geometry, start frame, motion or authorized audio. Use only references needed in the visible scene. Do not remove a required product view simply to reach an arbitrary low count.

Translate semantic labels into the provider's verified binding format. If the route uses `@imageN`, use that syntax exactly and reserve it for actual uploaded slots; do not invent `@Hero` or refer to an upload that does not exist. If it uses structured IDs or another syntax, follow that instead. Keep plain role names such as Guide, Room and Notebook distinct from provider IDs.

Example scope: “Room defines counter placement, doorway and wall geometry only. Guide defines the fictional presenter's identity and wardrobe. Notebook defines the supplied notebook's visible appearance.” A geometry reference does not automatically supply its lighting, occupants or camera angle.

Choose one approved identity authority per recurring subject, which may be a consistent multi-view sheet. Avoid competing images or textual descriptions that contradict it. Body text supplies action and behavior; it need not redundantly rewrite the face or product specification. Store canonical role definitions and approved identity notes in project state. Preserve them across attempts. Upload slot numbers may change; validate and update the adapter bindings rather than copying stale slot IDs verbatim.

## Audio, captions and CTA

Advertising may deliberately use voiceover, dialogue, licensed music, sound effects, captions, callouts and CTAs. Do not import the source's blanket “no narration,” “no music” or “no subtitles” defaults.

Keep audio layers separate in the plan, for example `[Voiceover]`, `[Dialogue]`, `[Music]`, `[Sound effects]` and `[Timed accents]`. Each has a purpose and exact cue or text where necessary. Ensure the message works muted through visuals and captions. Music supports speech rather than masking it; use only authorized recordings or properly licensed generated audio within the approved Higgsfield route. Do not assume native speech or audio editing is available until checked.

Keep graphic text as a controlled layer wherever possible. Use the exact copy manifest, modern licensed type and safe-area/contrast checks from [typography and UI/UX](typography-uiux.md). A translation may require new voice timing, caption wraps and lip-sync; it is not always a simple text replacement. Preserve accepted visual identity while testing the localized result at normal speed.

Make the CTA explicit in every final video. Give it a stable, readable hold chosen from actual copy length and playback review, not a universal duration. A quiet closing hold is purposeful: the new information is the next step. Do not allow automatic fades, rapidly moving backgrounds or interface overlays to erase it. If exact text assembly is unavailable, report the clean clip and pending text assembly separately; a clip without its required CTA is not final.

## Compile a clean production prompt

Keep the working plan, source evidence, approved copy, canonical references, capability response and job ledger in project files. Compile only instructions relevant to the model:

1. Active references and scoped roles in the verified provider syntax.
2. Purpose, first frame, relevant identity and physical invariants.
3. Coherent visual treatment, motivated light and local material color.
4. Necessary acting tasks and physical constraints.
5. Timed stages with framing, action, camera, end state and explicit transitions.
6. Intentional audio layers and approved text/overlay plan.
7. A short closing hold block restating the few essential invariants and final CTA treatment.

Do not include abandoned options, contradictory instructions, quality-score notes, internal QA labels, unused references or unsupported controls. Check prompt-field length and actual tool parameter limits. No fixed word count or reference quota. Preserve meaningful locks while removing repeated filler.

## Review and localized repair

Review the full returned clip with audio where available, not only thumbnails. Inspect key action transitions frame by frame when contact, identity or proof is at risk. Evaluate commercial clarity separately from technical validity. Record time ranges and the observed defect, not a vague verdict that it “looks AI.”

The independent slop-control reviewer checks: immediate message clarity, purposeful visual progression, reference consistency, hands/face/anatomy, contact/weight/physics, camera continuity, material color, legible exact text, natural delivery, useful sound, CTA and destination consistency. They must also identify attractive but generic footage that could sell any unrelated product.

Choose the smallest repair supported by the current route:

- **Local text/caption issue:** fix the controlled graphic layer; preserve approved video.
- **Local visual or audio issue:** use a verified scoped edit if available, with one named correction and explicit locks; otherwise replace the affected shot using the approved references and supported assembly.
- **Global identity, geography or concept failure:** correct the responsible plan/reference and regenerate the necessary unit. Repeated local patches cannot repair a globally wrong design.
- **A story that needs additional duration:** plan another supported unit or a verified extension. This is new scope and cost, not automatically a defect repair.

Change one causal defect or hypothesis per retry where possible; related consequences may need a coherent fix. Preserve working material. Log request, actual job ID, costs, inspected output, chosen correction and result. Use the main workflow's repair cap and spending authorization. Do not regenerate merely to see whether it becomes better.

## Original example: project-planning service ad

**Status:** an illustrative production recipe, not a submitted job or proven creative. Use it only for an advertiser whose actual service is a project-planning session and whose destination describes that service. Nothing in the scene claims to show a client result or the advertiser's real office.

**Plan:** one staged tabletop situation. Prepare an approved neutral room plate, a fictional Guide in Seedream 5, and a plain Notebook with three unmarked cards and one unbranded pen with its cap beside it in Nano Banana Pro if those elements need to remain consistent. Use the smallest needed reference set. Bind actual files through the runtime adapter. The proposed 15-second duration below must be supported by the connected route; otherwise adapt the units before submission.

**Production prompt:**

```text
ACTIVE REFERENCES
Reference A defines Room geometry and table placement only.
Reference B defines Guide identity and wardrobe only.
Reference C defines Notebook, the three plain cards and the plain pen only.
Use the verified uploaded-file bindings for A, B and C. They are references, not customer evidence.

Create a staged advertisement for a project-planning session. Guide sits at the table with Notebook open and three separate plain cards to its left. One uncapped pen and its cap lie separately to the Notebook's right. The viewer must read a small practical decision through the hand movements. Keep Guide, Room and Notebook consistent with the assigned references. No customer testimonial, business logo or invented result.

Soft daylight from frame left illuminates the table and Guide's hands. Keep off-white paper, natural skin and the table's pale wood in their own local colors. Preserve readable midtones and coherent contact shadows. The appearance is an ordinary observed workspace, with no decorative haze, exaggerated skin smoothing or cinematic color wash.

0–4s — Shot 1 — Medium close view across the table:
Hold the camera just above tabletop height. Guide looks from the open Notebook to the cards, then reaches toward the nearest card. The fingertip touches its edge before moving it. End state: the hand rests on that one card; all three cards remain visible and separate. No cut conceals the contact.

4–9s — Shot 2 — Continuous closer view of the same action:
Make one slow, shallow push toward the hands. Guide slides the selected card into the center of the open page, keeping it in contact with the paper. The other two cards stay in place. Guide releases the card and lightly taps its top edge once. End state: one card lies on the page, two remain to the left, and both hands are clear of the selected card.

9–12s — Shot 3 — Cut to a slightly wider view from the same side:
Guide looks at the selected card, picks up the pen and cap from the right, closes the cap with visible contact, and places the pen back beside the Notebook. Keep the page and cards unchanged. End state: a settled tabletop with the selected card still readable as a distinct object and clear space for the closing copy.

12–15s — Shot 4 — Hold the accepted wider composition:
Guide's hands stay at rest. Keep the table stable and the copy area unobstructed. Preserve the scene for the exact graphic CTA added in finishing; do not invent physical writing on the cards.

[Voiceover, 0–10s]
“A project in mind? Explore the planning session. See what's included and choose your next step.”
[Sound effects]
Quiet room tone, a light paper slide at contact, and one soft pen-cap click. No extra spoken words.
[Graphic text for controlled finishing]
0–4s: “Where do I start?”
9–12s: “Explore the planning session”
12–15s: “See what's included”
Keep the final CTA continuously readable. Match it to the real service-information destination.

HOLD FOR THE FULL TIMELINE
One Guide and the approved Room, Notebook and three cards retain their identities.
The selected card moves through visible contact; the other two stay at the left.
All exact wording comes from the approved voiceover and graphic text manifest.
The ending leaves a stable, unobstructed CTA area; no invented endorsement or result.
```

Before submission replace the example reference labels with verified bindings, select only supported audio/text controls and separate finishing instructions from the generation prompt if those instructions belong to assembly. If native voiceover is unavailable, use an authorized supported audio route or deliver that component as pending; do not claim a silent clip contains narration.

**Acceptance:** first four seconds communicate a decision; exactly one card moves and its contact remains visible; hand, pen and paper geometry stay coherent; cuts preserve positions; the voiceover sounds natural without extra words; the ad works muted; CTA is readable through its actual hold; landing page matches the stated planning session. Reject it if the footage suggests documented client work, fabricated service results or a first-person customer endorsement. Compare this concept with materially different buyer insights in a test; its existence does not establish conversion.
