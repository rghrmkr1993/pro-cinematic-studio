# Rights, chain of title, AI ownership and releases

Not legal advice. A qualified lawyer reviews and finalises every document. Labels: (F) from a primary or official source, (3P) law-firm or broker article, (E) estimate, (A) assumption, (U) unverified.

## 1. Chain of title
Chain of title is the documented trail proving the producer owns or controls every right needed to exhibit the film. Distributors and insurers check it before acquisition or coverage (3P: broker and law-firm guides 2026).
Typical package (3P, US-centred; adapt to Indian contracts with counsel):
1. Script: author or writer agreement with assignment of copyright (work for hire or assignment), or an option and purchase agreement for adapted material.
2. Underlying rights: licence for any book, story, play, song lyric, article, life story or real event presented as fact; public-domain claims backed by research (U unless checked per country and edition).
3. Crew and creative agreements with assignment: director, editor, composer, colourist, designers, AI operators, freelancers.
4. Performer agreements with likeness and voice terms; separate digital-replica clauses (section 5).
5. Music: composer agreement transferring score ownership, sync and master licences for each licensed track (music-and-insurance.md).
6. Location, property, artwork, logo and trademark clearances.
7. Tool and platform terms: generator licences and any restrictions on commercial use, training and ownership of output (higgsfield-master help-center-facts.md); keep dated copies.
8. Copyright registration of the film and script where available (India: Copyright Office registration is evidentiary, not a precondition (A); US: needed before suit, 3P).
9. Title report or search: confirm no conflicting registered or well-known title (3P).
10. Legal opinion or chain-of-title memo from counsel: some platforms require it before delivery (3P, Netflix cited by broker guides; re-check with the platform).
Common defects: unsigned script assignments, missing minor releases, music cleared for festivals only, licences that predate streaming (3P). Distributors often allow a cure window after a defect surfaces (3P: about 30 days; contract-specific).

## 2. Script and story rights
- Original script: written by a human or developed with documented human authorship; keep drafts and dates.
- Adaptation: option agreement (short term, small fee) then purchase; scope covers medium, territory, language, term, sequels.
- Real events, real people: life rights where depiction may defame or intrude; consult counsel; no real person without consent.
- Mythology and epics: texts may be public domain, but modern retellings, designs, translations and songs carry their own rights; religious depictions carry regulatory and community-sensitivity risk (CBFC flags).
- Fan work and parody: not a safe harbour; avoid.

## 3. Ownership of AI-generated content (summary as of Oct 2026; counsel to confirm)
India (3P, law-firm and academic commentary read 2026-10-14):
- Section 2(d)(vi) of the Copyright Act 1957 (inserted 1994) names "the person who causes the work to be created" as author of computer-generated literary, dramatic, musical or artistic works. Commentary: AI is not the author; the clause predates generative AI; who "causes" the work is contested (prompter, developer); originality needs skill and judgment; no Indian court has settled the position for purely AI-generated output; reform and the ANI v OpenAI case (Delhi High Court, pending) are being watched; commentary differs, so verify against primary sources (U).
- Cinematograph film authorship: the producer is the first owner of the film copyright under the Act (3P, producer documentation guide); this does not by itself resolve AI-generated frames.
- Practical rule: treat AI output as low-certainty IP; add human authorship you can prove: written script, designed boards, selection and arrangement, editing, sound, music by a human composer or a licence.
US (F and 3P):
- Thaler v Perlmutter: the D.C. Circuit held human authorship is required (Mar 2025); the Supreme Court denied certiorari on 2 Mar 2026, which is not a merits ruling; the question of how much human input suffices is still open (3P).
- Copyright Office Part 2 report (Jan 2025): prompts alone do not give authorship; protectable are human-authored expression, selection and arrangement, modifications (as in film-studio delivery-and-legal.md section 6.9).
- Registration: disclose AI-generated material and disclaim it; claim only the human contribution (A: confirm the current Copyright Office registration guidance).
Other territories: UK has a computer-generated works provision, EU and others differ; counsel for each target market.
Our standing practice: evidence file (script drafts, boards, edit decisions, prompts with dates), human-composed or licensed music, human-led edit, and a statement of what was AI-generated.

## 4. Third-party platform and tool terms
Read the generator terms for ownership of output, commercial use, training rights, indemnity and confidentiality. Some terms give the vendor a licence to content. Do not upload unreleased scripts or client assets without client approval and a terms check. Keep dated copies of terms in the rights file.

## 5. Release templates (starting outlines for counsel; fill, do not improvise legal wording)
General: parties, project title (working title ok), date, definitions, grant, scope, term, territory, media (all media now known or later devised, 3P), compensation, credit, warranties, no obligation to use, moral rights note (India: counsel), governing law and jurisdiction, stamp duty and execution formalities per state (E: counsel), signature and witness, guardian signature for minors.

### 5.1 Model or performer release
Person and role; grant to photograph, record, depict and use likeness and performance in the film, trailers, marketing and archives; AI scope: whether the performer is filmed, scanned, voiced or replaced by a synthetic stand-in, and whether any generative model is trained on their materials (separate sign-off); compensation; approval rights on sensitive uses (nudity, politics, sensitive content); revocation and deletion; data protection note: images and voice are personal data (India: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, counsel to confirm).

### 5.2 Voice release (including digital replica)
Separate lines for voice, name, signature and face (3P). Define "digital replica" to cover any synthetic version of the voice. State: the technology in plain terms; the named project, language, platform, territory, term and renewal; compensation; output approval; no training of general models on raw audio without a separate signature; deletion of recordings and trained models on request or at term end; no sublicence or reuse without new consent and fee; revocation mechanics (3P, voice-actor contract guidance, mostly US). For Indian voice artists, counsel adds personality rights and moral rights points (courts have granted personality rights against AI voice misuse, 3P, U).

### 5.3 Location and property release
Full address and description; dates and hours; areas included and excluded; set modifications; liability and damage terms; permission to show in the film and marketing; signer's authority; a photo walkthrough before the shoot to record condition (3P). For AI-generated locations modelled on real places: record the real place used as reference and whether the building, artwork or trademark is recognisable; avoid recognisable private property or protected landmarks without a release or counsel's view.

### 5.4 Music and sound
Composer agreement (assignment or licence, credit, royalties, PRO split), sync and master licence template (music-and-insurance.md), library licence record, sample and loop licences, cue sheet.

### 5.5 Minors, extras, crowd
Guardian consent and counsel; no minors in generated sensitive scenes; extras sign a short release; crowd of AI-generated people needs no release but must not resemble real identifiable persons (E).

### 5.6 Contributor and crew assignment
Work-for-hire or assignment of rights for every contributor; moral rights waiver or consent as counsel advises; credit terms; confidentiality; tool use rules (generative tools allowed, disallowed).

## 6. Consent file structure
`ledgers/consents/<person>/` with signed PDF, ID check note (A: keep minimal personal data), scope summary line, expiry date, withdrawal log. A one-page register lists person, role, items used, scope, term, territory, fee, status. Producer checks the register against the credits roll.
