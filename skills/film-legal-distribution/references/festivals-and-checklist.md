# Festival strategy, AI festivals and the release checklist

Not legal advice. Rules and deadlines change every year: read each festival's current rules page and form. Labels: (F) official, (3P) secondary, (E) estimate, (A) assumption, (U) unverified.

## 1. Festival strategy (cheapest, most honest route first)
1. Define the goal: audience, reviews, distributor interest, awards eligibility, a calling card for the next film, or an AI-film showcase. Different goals pick different festivals.
2. Tier plan (E): 1 or 2 reach festivals, 4 to 6 realistic festivals including regional Indian and diaspora festivals, 2 to 4 AI-specific or genre festivals; submit in waves by deadline.
3. Rules matrix per festival: runtime limits, premiere status (world, national, regional), online-availability rules (a public YouTube upload can kill premiere status), language and subtitle rules, AI-use disclosure and eligibility, fees and waivers, format (online screener, DCP), notification dates.
4. Premiere discipline: do not release online before the festival run if premiere status matters; keep the film private or unlisted until the strategy says otherwise (the user decides).
5. Budget: submission fees (E: often 20 to 100 USD each, varies), DCP, subtitles, poster and press kit, travel, E&O if a sales agent asks. Spend needs the user's yes.
6. Materials: film link or file, poster, stills, trailer, synopsis (short, long), director's statement including how AI was used, cast and crew list with AI roles named, music list, rights statement, subtitle files, bios supplied by the people, contact; keep one master document.
7. Submission platforms: festival portals and aggregators (3P: FilmFreeway and similar); the user creates accounts and pays; agents prepare materials only.
8. Track: festival, deadline, fee, status, notes in production-tracking (a simple CSV is enough).
9. After acceptance: DCP or screener per spec, subtitle QC, publicity kit, laurel usage rules (only real laurels), screening time and Q and A plan, a lawyer check on any distribution terms offered.

## 2. AI disclosure at festivals (3P, Aug 2026 articles; verify each form)
- Expect to disclose AI use, by stage (pre-production, generation, post). Wording varies: Sundance wording is broad (any use, even denoise and upscale); TIFF and Berlinale expect disclosure; SXSW has an AI category; some AI-specific festivals require generative tools; Tribeca sources conflict, read the form (as noted in film-studio delivery-and-legal.md).
- A director's statement on how AI was used is expected; include human authorship, tools, what was filmed or generated, and consent for any likeness or voice.
- Oscars (99th, 3P): AI use is neither a help nor a hindrance; human authorship is weighed; acting performances must be demonstrably performed by humans with consent to be eligible (so fully synthetic acting is unlikely to qualify). Eligibility also needs qualifying runs and rules outside this skill (U).
- Indian film awards and national festivals: rules on AI vary and may be silent (U); ask the organiser in writing and keep the answer.

## 3. AI film festivals and showcases (U: names and dates must be verified; rules change quickly)
- Several festivals and competitions run AI-specific categories or whole programmes (for example AI film competitions run by generative-video vendors and independent AI film festivals). Treat each as an unverified lead until you read its rules.
- Questions to check: who owns or may use submitted films (some competitions take a licence to show or promote), whether tools are restricted, whether the vendor's credits or watermarks are required, prize terms and payment, content rules, how consent and likeness are handled, public vote mechanics.
- Risk: a licence grant to the organiser can conflict with exclusivity to a platform or distributor; counsel reads the terms before submission.
- Value: AI festivals give visibility within the AI community; traditional festivals give industry access. Mix per goal, and be clear in the film's materials about what was generated.

## 4. Release checklist (producer owns; each line has an owner and a date)
Legal and rights
- [ ] Chain-of-title file complete; lawyer review recorded
- [ ] Script and story rights papers (original or option and purchase)
- [ ] Contributor assignments signed (crew, composer, designers, AI operators)
- [ ] Performer and voice releases signed; digital replica clauses; minors with guardian consent
- [ ] Location, property, logo and artwork clearances (or fictional replacements)
- [ ] Music: originals assigned; library and licensed tracks have sync and master licences; cue sheet complete; IPRS and PPL position noted
- [ ] Fonts, stock, tools: licences logged; generator terms reviewed and saved
- [ ] Title search done; no conflicting title
- [ ] E&O quote requested if buyer or agent needs it
- [ ] AI-use statement drafted; platform and festival disclosures planned
Certification
- [ ] CBFC decision: needed (theatre) or online only (counsel confirmed)
- [ ] CBFC dossier: application, synopsis, script with lyrics, credits, format details, subtitles, music licences, accessibility, fee
- [ ] Target category chosen; cut list prepared
Delivery
- [ ] Master archived with checksum; textless and M and E elements
- [ ] Platform exports passed QC on the real files; loudness reported
- [ ] Subtitle and caption files QC'd (Indic fonts checked, native review)
- [ ] DCP made and tested (if festival or theatre)
- [ ] Credits approved; spelling diff against consent ledger
Marketing
- [ ] Poster, thumbnails, trailer, stills, press kit (title-and-key-art); no unlicensed imagery
- [ ] Metadata sheet complete; claims truthful; laurels real
Approvals
- [ ] The user's explicit yes before any publish, submission, upload or send
- [ ] Nothing sent or uploaded by an agent
- [ ] Log updated; handoff note current

## 5. Calendar model (E, adjust per project)
T-12 weeks: consents, rights, music plan. T-10: picture lock plan, CBFC prep. T-8: credits, music licences signed. T-6: festival submissions wave 1, E&O quote. T-4: DCP and subtitle QC, CBFC filing (allow 2x the stated examination time). T-2: poster, trailer, press kit final. T-1: QC on real files, final checklist. T-0: release only on the user's yes.
