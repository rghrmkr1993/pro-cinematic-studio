# Visual language: scene, story, color, mood, light, camera (researched 2026-10-13)
Labels: (V) vendor-official, (3P) third-party/practitioner, (H) house rule from earlier company research. Use as vocabulary; models respond to concrete, physical terms more than adjectives.

## Scene construction
Order every shot: Subject, Action (start and end pose), Environment (3 fixed anchors), Camera (one move), Light (one named source), Style/grade, Constraints. Frame distance bands: ELS, WS, MS, MCU, CU, ECU, insert. Adjacent cuts must differ in POV, distance and action or they morph (H).
Snippet: "Medium close-up, 50mm, woman in linen shirt lifts a glass dropper to the light, then lowers it to her palm. Bathroom with north-facing window, white tile, wooden shelf. Locked-off camera, no zoom. Soft window light from left. Natural color, fine grain."

## Story beats (15-30s ad)
0-1.5s hook (mid-motion, tension or sensory macro) | 1.5-5s problem or desire | 5-15s product moment (one action per beat) | 15-22s proof (texture, result honestly, real review text) | last 2-3s CTA and end card. Brand film (60-90s): setting, desire, obstacle, discovery, transformation, signature image. Shot list columns: #, duration, distance, camera move, action, light, audio, reference Elements.

## Color grading and palette language
- Palette words: warm ivory and clay, botanical green and brass, bone and charcoal, sea-glass teal, soft terracotta, sage and cream, midnight and amber.
- Named looks: clean natural (neutral white balance, lifted shadows, soft roll-off), warm film (halation, gentle grain, amber highlights), bleach bypass (low saturation, high contrast), teal-orange (blockbuster, avoid for skincare), high-key beauty (bright, low contrast, minimal shadows), low-key noir (deep blacks, single source).
- Specify: white balance (neutral, warm 5200K), contrast (soft or punchy), saturation (muted, rich), highlight handling (roll-off, bloom), grain (fine 35mm, none). Use hex palettes via Soul HEX or Cinema 50+ palette templates; Cinema 4.0 grade controls for temperature, contrast, saturation, grain, highlights, exposure (V).
- Grade the hero frame first, match the rest to it. Same grade across a campaign (H).

## Mood and light vocabulary
- Light sources: window light, overcast daylight, golden-hour backlight, open shade, practicals (lamp, candle), neon, overhead fall, contre-jour, soft cross, silhouette (Cinema lighting presets, V).
- Quality: soft, diffused, hard, specular, dappled, bounce, rim, kicker. Ratios: high-key, low-key. Motivation: always name the source and direction ("soft window light from camera left").
- Mood to light: calm and trust, soft diffuse daylight, muted greens; luxury, low-key with warm rim and slow moves; energy, hard light, saturated, fast cuts (but only one element fast); clinical, clean sharp lens, cool neutral, deep focus; nostalgia, vintage lens, halation, 8mm film.
- Default for UGC and skincare: one motivated neutral source, consistent WB, deep focus, never studio strobe or sunset cliche unless asked (H).

## Camera language
Moves: static, handheld (documentary, UGC), push in, pull back, dolly left or right, truck, arc or orbit, crane or jib up and down, tilt, pan, whip pan, pedestal, tracking follow, POV, drone, 360 roll. Seedance reads dolly in, truck left, arc shot, push in, pull back wide, handheld follow, crane up, orbital (V).
Rules: one move per shot, single direction, smooth, state what camera is NOT doing ("no cuts, no zoom"); specify speed as a value ("slow, roughly 1 meter over 4 seconds"); put energy in the edit.
Lens: 14mm wide environments, 24-35mm natural, 50mm portrait, 85mm compression, macro for texture. Aperture: f/1.4 isolation, f/4 balanced, f/11 deep.
Snippets: "Macro insert, extreme close-up of serum drop falling from dropper onto fingertip, slow, shallow focus, soft side light, no camera movement." "Slow push in from medium to close-up over 6 seconds, subject still, eyes to lens."

## Texture and product beauty vocabulary
Cream: whipped, velvety, ribbon, peaks. Serum: glass droplet, viscous strand, translucent amber. Oil: golden, glossy sheen. Soap or balm: matte, grain visible, cut face. Botanicals: dew, stems, petals, seeds. Surfaces: stone, linen, oak, ceramic. Keep any texture shown honest to the real product.

## Negative tail (video)
"no text, no watermark, no logos except the product label, no extra hands, no morphing, no duplicate product, no cartoon, no 3D look, no beauty filter, no flicker."

## Pacing for Reels, Shorts, ads
15-30s total; hook inside 1s; cut every 1.5-3s for energetic, 3-4s for calm; speed ramp sparingly; loop-friendly final frame; captions in safe zone; audio: one clear voice or ambient plus light bed, no lyrics.

## Official additions [official, 2026-10-13]
Camera blog (2026-10-09): a move is start, end, speed curve and a changing lens-subject relation; state them. Aperture f/1.4 isolates, f/4 moderate, f/11 deep focus; lens character (Clean Sharp, Anamorphic, Vintage Anamorphic, Warm Vintage, Halation Vintage) changes the footage as much as the move. Academy: 60:30:10 colour rule, contre-jour backlight with camera on the shadow side, 180 degree shutter, Dutch angle quoted in degrees, handheld as measured tremor, speed ramps for freeze beats, match cut on a frozen pose.
