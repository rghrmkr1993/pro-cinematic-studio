# 12 Signature Cinematic Shots
Adapted from an infographic by @BEGINNERSBLOG; prompts rewritten.
Use: pick one shot, then follow the Higgsfield tip. Confirm model names and camera options on the Generate button; the Cinema Studio 4.0 Camera panel offers Auto, Modern, 35mm Film, 8mm Film, DV Camcorder bodies and the lens and aperture presets listed in cinema-studio.md. Where a shot needs a lens the panel lacks, build it with the nearest preset and describe the look in the prompt.

## 1. Ground-level forced perspective
- Camera position: about 8 cm off the ground, tilted up 70-90 degrees.
- Lens: 14-24mm.
- Why use it: a subject looms over the frame and reads as powerful.
- Best for: hero, action, fashion, urban.
- Prompt: Camera rests almost on the pavement and tilts up toward a figure standing over it, coat hem stirring, towers rising behind, flat daylight.
- Higgsfield tip: Camera type 35mm Film, lens Clean Sharp, aperture f/4, one slow tilt-up move. Suits a video model for the tilt; a still image model works for a single hero frame.
- Risk: stretched anatomy. Hands and feet nearest the lens balloon, and the face drifts during the tilt.

## 2. Fisheye proximity
- Camera position: 20-50 cm from the subject.
- Lens: 8-16mm fisheye.
- Why use it: bends the frame and adds raw energy.
- Best for: music videos, fashion, street.
- Prompt: The lens sits an arm's length from a dancer who turns into it, the frame edges curving like a barrel, a quick snap of the head.
- Higgsfield tip: Camera type 8mm Film for the distortion, aperture f/4, handheld movement preset. Video model only, since the energy comes from motion.
- Risk: face warping at the frame edges, and product labels or hands bending out of shape.

## 3. Porthole wide
- Camera position: centre of the scene, looking all the way around.
- Lens: 4-8mm, circular frame.
- Why use it: shows the whole environment in one shot.
- Best for: world-building, surreal worlds.
- Prompt: The camera hangs in the middle of a plaza, a round frame holding market stalls on every side, then turns slowly.
- Higgsfield tip: no circular lens in the panel, so generate the 360 environment as a locked still (Soul Location or Nano Banana Pro), then animate a slow Pan with 8mm Film. Still image model for the environment, video model for the turn.
- Risk: seams where the wrap meets, figures repeated around the rim, and location drift during rotation.

## 4. Dream lens with smeared edges
- Camera position: 1-2 m from the subject.
- Lens: vintage or Deakinizer-style, sharp centre with smeared edges.
- Why use it: memories and flashbacks feel soft and distant.
- Best for: flashbacks, nostalgia, memory scenes.
- Prompt: A woman in a doorway stays crisp in the middle of the frame while the edges melt into warm light, a slow push toward her.
- Higgsfield tip: lens Halation Vintage or Warm Vintage, aperture f/1.4 to isolate the subject, slow push-in. Still image model (Soul Cinema) for the look, video model for the flashback sequence.
- Risk: smeared edges can hide hands or product, and the face drifts when the subject moves toward the blurred edge.

## 5. Tunnel vision
- Camera position: from the subject's viewpoint or a close front view.
- Lens: standard lens with a centre-spot diopter look.
- Why use it: radial blur builds psychological tension.
- Best for: fear, trauma, altered perception.
- Prompt: Everything outside a small bright circle collapses into soft radial smear as the character breathes hard and holds still.
- Higgsfield tip: Genre Horror, Tempo Single Shot, aperture f/1.4, Emotion Wheel set to Fear on the character through an @-mention. Video model, because the blur narrows over time.
- Risk: the subject becomes unreadable if the vignette is too dark, and the blur can look like a rendering glitch. Keep the scene non-graphic.

## 6. Dual-focus
- Camera position: close to the subject, angled composition.
- Lens: 35-50mm with a swing or shift look.
- Why use it: foreground and background are both sharp, so tension stays in view.
- Best for: suspense, subjective scenes, product plus model.
- Prompt: A bottle sits sharp in the foreground while a model in the back of the room stays equally sharp, the camera drifting slowly sideways.
- Higgsfield tip: Camera type 35mm Film, aperture f/11 for deep focus, lens Clean Sharp, slow lateral dolly. Still image model for the composition; video model if the drift matters.
- Risk: deep focus shows every seam in the background, and product label text turns to gibberish. Overlay copy in edit instead.

## 7. Eye reflection macro
- Camera position: extreme close-up on the eye.
- Lens: 50-100mm macro.
- Why use it: a small reflection in the cornea carries emotion, confrontation or a reveal.
- Best for: emotional beats, confrontation, reveal moments.
- Prompt: A tight shot of one eye, the iris filling the frame, a tiny figure visible in the reflection, a barely perceptible push-in.
- Higgsfield tip: lens Clean Sharp, aperture f/1.4, slow push-in. Still image model (Soul Cinema or Nano Banana Pro) for the reveal frame; video model for the push. Use a consented face only.
- Risk: the reflection geometry is wrong, the two eyes disagree, and eyelashes or the iris go asymmetric.

## 8. Macro-to-world
- Camera position: starts in extreme macro, then pulls back.
- Lens: 24mm probe lens.
- Why use it: a close detail opens into the full scale of the world.
- Best for: product reveals, transitions.
- Prompt: Starting on the texture of a product surface, the camera pulls back steadily until the whole bathroom and routine come into view.
- Higgsfield tip: video model. Name the move as a Pull Out or Dolly Out preset with start and end framing in the prompt, lens Clean Sharp, aperture f/11 so the macro and wide both stay sharp.
- Risk: morphing at the handoff between texture and room, and product label or shape drifting as the frame widens.

## 9. Periscope / impossible axis
- Camera position: moves through tight spaces, sideways or rotating.
- Lens: periscope look, 24-35mm.
- Why use it: impossible moves feel premium and surprising.
- Best for: luxury and product shots, transitions.
- Prompt: The camera slides low along a shelf, rotates past a bottle, then slides out the other side, one smooth arc.
- Higgsfield tip: one move only, a Robot Arm or POV preset, lens Anamorphic for a widescreen luxury feel. The "Impossible Camera" MoveSet is a 3.5 name, so describe the move in the prompt instead. Video model.
- Risk: geometry breaks at the turn, product splits or duplicates, and objects clip through walls.

## 10. Free-lens distortion
- Camera position: handheld, unstable.
- Lens: free-lensing (lens whacking) with light leaks.
- Why use it: unstable frames suggest hallucination or a disturbed mind.
- Best for: psychological scenes, dreams, transitions.
- Prompt: A handheld camera jolts and swings past a figure in a dim room, warm light leaks flaring across the frame.
- Higgsfield tip: Camera type DV Camcorder, lens Halation Vintage, Genre Drama, Tempo Chaotic. Video model only.
- Risk: flicker, identity drift between shakes, and extra limbs when the motion is too random. Keep the prompt short.

## 11. Mirror doppelganger
- Camera position: front or side angle that includes a mirror.
- Lens: 35-50mm.
- Why use it: the reflection shows a different version of the subject.
- Best for: thrillers, duality, identity themes.
- Prompt: A woman faces a tall mirror; her reflection wears a darker jacket and raises a hand a beat before she does.
- Higgsfield tip: build the reflection as a second Element or use Edit Video on a region to change wardrobe in the reflection, aperture f/4. Still image model for the first frame, video for the timed reflection beat. Never use a real person's likeness for the second figure without documented consent.
- Risk: the reflection copies the wrong pose, face drift between original and reflection, and mirror-edge artifacts.

## 12. Fragmented reflection
- Camera position: through fractured glass or a prism.
- Lens: 50-85mm.
- Why use it: one face stays sharp while the others refract into fragments.
- Best for: fashion, music videos, dream sequences.
- Prompt: A face sits sharp at the centre of a cracked pane while split copies of the same face refract outward in colour bands.
- Higgsfield tip: lens Clean Sharp, aperture f/1.4, with a still image model (Nano Banana Pro) to compose the glass overlay first. Video model if the refraction should shift.
- Risk: the sharp face gets refracted by mistake, duplicated faces break anatomy, and glass edges cut through features.

## Pairing for ads
Skincare and product use only. Never show a before-and-after result or a claim the product cannot support; overlay copy in edit, and follow compliance.md.
- Macro-to-world: a product reveal that opens from a texture close-up into the full routine.
- Eye reflection macro: an eye-cream ad, using a consented model and no result claims.
- Dual-focus: a product in the foreground with the model sharp behind it, for a bottle plus face campaign.
- Ground-level forced perspective: a hero launch where the bottle towers over the scene.
- Fragmented reflection: a prism-glass bottle or a pearlescent jar for a fashion-led beauty drop.
- Alternate-camera cut (creator blog 2026-09-16): one static master take becomes 13 angles in 30 s. Attach the take as a video reference; lock cast, seats, dialogue and timing; list hard cuts with exact seconds and a camera per shot (dolly back, true overhead, across glassware, bottle-mounted low angle, inside the glass, over-shoulder, focus pull across three layers, grass-level pet close-up). Output time equals source time. Risk: seat drift between cuts and invented actions; end the prompt with a no-extra-cuts, no-invented-action line.
- Previz one-take (creator blog 2026-09-12): block grey shapes in Blender or 3D Jutsu, export the blocking video, repaint with Seedance 2.5 video-to-video using an opening frame and a character sheet. Map placeholder colours to real objects, state crowd counts and facing codes, verify loops numerically. Risk: the repaint copies the grey look; say the blocking defines camera and timing only.
