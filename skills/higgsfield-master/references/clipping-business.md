# Clipping business: repurposing long video into short edits for income (researched 2026-10-13)
Source: creator blog by @adilinthewildtempo on Higgsfield, published 2026-10-11 (Katana post), plus the $5K, ads and influencer posts by the same creator. Labels: (V) vendor or creator claim, (E) our estimate, (A) assumption, (U) unverified. Not legal or tax advice. Client-neutral.

## 1. What the post teaches
- A clip is a short vertical edit cut from a longer film, podcast, stream or recording. Each edit needs 3 separate inputs: source footage, an editing reference video (sets pacing, shot rhythm, transitions) and a music track. Reuse the same reference and music direction across a batch so it feels like one series (V).
- Tools named: Higgsfield Katana (preset edit styles: aura, motion, clipping) run through Claude with the Higgsfield MCP; the "Higgsfield Edit Studio" plugin v0.3.0 (zip, linked from the post); Claude Opus 5.5 selected in chat; type /katana to open presets (V). The same plugin is now installed here as edit-director, edit-voice and edit-gen (section 8).
- The creator frames this as sample-first: one edit from your own footage becomes the portfolio piece that opens a client conversation.

## 2. Workflow end to end
1. Source: own footage, or material you have permission to edit and publish. Podcast needs clear speech. Action or transformation content needs the subject and payoff to survive a 9:16 crop.
2. Rights model: written permission, a client brief, or an official clipping program with posted terms. No permission, no clip (section 5).
3. Clip selection: pick one strong, self-contained moment (a statement that makes sense alone, one character, one visual payoff). The opening must be understandable without the full film.
4. Pick an editing reference whose style fits the source; study what viewers respond to (opening, timing, one moment). Use footage from the source only, never from the reference.
5. Prompt the edit with 4 facts: duration, format (9:16), the moment to build on, and the role of the music. Ask for a short report after analysis, edit plan and preview, so you can steer before more versions.
6. Captions and hooks: readable captions inside the platform safe areas, motion graphics only where they explain or stress the statement, speech kept clear over the music, cuts timed to the drop.
7. Batch: after the first edit direction is approved, ask for 10 different 25-30 s edits from the same source, each with a different moment and hook, exported separately in 9:16 (V).
8. Variants: a Korean (or other language) version of an approved edit, with names and factual claims preserved, subtitle timing relaid, a native-speaker check flagged; a 30 s teaser from a film that withholds the ending.
9. Deliver, then iterate on client feedback; offer a recurring package.

## 3. The six prompt types in the post (paraphrased shape)
| Type | Length | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Film aura edit | 25-30 s | 1 character, strong line or reaction, reference for pacing, music drop carries the best moment, dialogue stays clear, 9:16 safe areas |
| Podcast aura edit | 25-30 s | 1 self-contained statement, original voice and meaning preserved, captions, graphics, supporting visuals |
| Everyday footage Reel | 20-25 s | workout, travel or transformation; one visual payoff, purposeful zooms and text, subject stays in the vertical frame, no unrelated footage |
| Batch of 10 | 25-30 s each | follow-up in the same chat, new moment and hook per clip, same reference and music |
| Language version | as source | translate meaning, keep names and claims, relay subtitles, client-approved dubbing option only |
| Film teaser | 30 s | hook, context, rising stakes, unresolved last beat, no twists; explain what is withheld; report after each stage |
Common line in all prompts: use the clipping skill plus the Higgsfield MCP, attach reference and music separately, ask for a plan and preview before final.

## 4. Where the money comes from (and what is claimed)
- Routes named: campaign platforms with posted briefs and payment terms; direct outreach to creators or businesses whose content you understand; a public portfolio; agencies that need clip production. Pick an industry you already understand (gaming, sports, music, beauty, business).

## 5. Reality check
- The $3,000 per month figure is the post's own planning example (6 clients x $500). The post itself says it is not reported earnings or an average result. Treat it as a vendor and creator claim, not verified, and not a forecast (V, U).
- Unknowns that decide real income: client acquisition rate, churn, price pressure from other editors, platform reach, how long each clip takes after revisions, and whether you have rights. No cost per clip, no hours per clip and no client list are given in the post.
- Estimate for planning (E): code-rendered edits (edit-director, ffmpeg) cost 0 Higgsfield credits; generated B-roll or inserts add credits (Seedance 2.0 is about 23 per 5 s at 720p, credits-and-budget.md); Katana per-edit credit cost is not published, so read it on the Generate button. Time per finished clip, including review and revisions, is likely 30 to 90 minutes once the process is set (A, test on 3 clips before quoting).
- Income from views is slow: 100,000 eligible views per clip is a high bar for a new page (A).

## 6. Compliance notes (not legal advice; confirm with the Manager)
- Clip only content you own, content you have written permission for, or content from official clipping programs whose terms allow it. Clipping someone else's stream, film or podcast without permission can be copyright infringement and a platform takedown, even if you add captions.
- Meta (Instagram, Facebook) and YouTube both demote or demonetize unoriginal or reused content: repurposing needs real transformation (commentary, edit, new value), not re-uploads. YouTube's reused-content rule can remove monetisation from a channel; confirm current wording in each platform's policy page before launch (3P summary, U).
- Disclose AI-made or AI-altered realistic content: Meta AI info label and YouTube altered-or-synthetic content control. Do not fake a speaker saying words they did not say; keep original meaning in podcast edits.
- Dubbing or voice cloning a real person needs their written consent (compliance.md).
- TikTok is banned in India: do not plan, post or budget TikTok for India work. Use Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts and Facebook Reels.
- Never publish, message a client, take payment or spend credits without the user's explicit yes.

## 7. How our agency could run this (proposal, A, needs the founder's go)
- Owner: revenue-strategist owns the offer, pricing and pipeline; creative-director owns the edit style and QA; growth-marketer owns platform posting plans; research-analyst owns the weekly program and policy scan.
- Weekly cadence: Monday program and policy scan plus source and permission check; Tue-Thu edit 1 sample then batches with the Katana or edit-director path; Thursday QA (captions, safe areas, claims, AI label); Friday revenue review and next-week brief. Report to the Manager in 10 lines or fewer.
- KPIs (targets set by the founder, E): permitted sources signed; clips delivered per week; first-pass approval rate; revisions per batch; credits per delivered clip; hours per clip; paid packages and MRR; policy flags (target zero).
- Pilot: 1 sandbox source we own, 10 clips, measure hours and credits before any outreach. Client data and brands stay isolated per client.
