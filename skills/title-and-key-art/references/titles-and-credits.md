# Titles and credits

Labels: (F) from a source, (3P) secondary, (E) estimate, (A) assumption. Not legal advice. Contracts and guild or union agreements override every convention below.

## 1. Opening titles: the job
- Establish tone, world or theme in seconds, then get out of the film's way. Concept first: find a metaphor for the film, then the type (3P, Art of the Title case studies such as Split, Se7en, Watchmen).
- Minimal can win: a black screen, one typeface and precise timing can carry a thriller (3P, Split). Tactile or hand-made textures add character (3P, Se7en). Kinetic type has a long history (3P, North by Northwest is often cited as an early extended example).
- Designer and director work closely; the director has final say (3P).

## 2. Types of opening
| Type | Use | Length (E) |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open then main title | Most dramas; story hook before any card | title card at 1 to 5 min |
| Full title sequence | Genre films, series, anthologies | 60 to 150 s feature, 20 to 45 s series |
| Title card only | Shorts, festival films, ads | 3 to 6 s |
| Over-picture credits | Minimal approach, credits sit on live action | 30 to 90 s |
| Logos and legal first | Production and distribution logos precede | 5 to 15 s total |

## 3. Opening card order (convention (3P); the contract wins)
Production company logos, then an optional presenting line, then lead cast, then the title (position varies), then key crew: casting, music, costume, editor, production designer, director of photography, producers, writer, director last or just before the title. Keep to the approved billing block. For AI films add the AI-use line where the contract, platform or festival asks (film-legal-distribution).

## 4. Method for an AI-made title sequence
1. Write the narrative job and the motif (one sentence each).
2. Pick 1 or 2 motifs that can be built from plates already in the film (locations, props, textures) so the sequence feels native.
3. Storyboard on 6 to 10 frames with timecodes; mark the card for every credit.
4. Source imagery: reuse film plates, or generate text-free abstract plates (Higgsfield stills or short loops, quoted first). Keep them in the film's palette and grade.
5. Build in code: motion-reel for deterministic frames and beat-synced sound, or hyperframes for an HTML timeline. Type is live text, never generated.
6. Time to the music: hold each name on screen at least 2 to 4 s for readability (E); lead names can be longer.
7. Render ProRes 4444 with alpha for the conform if the editor will overlay on picture, or a flattened clip. Keep a text-free version.
8. Hand to the editor for the final conform; confirm frame rate and timecode with the colorist's master.

## 5. End credits: structure (3P, vendor and guild-derived guides)
Order, top to bottom:
1. Above-the-line cards, often one card each: director, writer(s), producer(s), executive producer(s), lead cast, supporting cast. Then director of photography, production designer, editor, associate producers, costume designer, composer, casting.
2. Scrolling credits: production management and assistant directors, full cast (order of appearance or as contracted), then crew by department, head first. Post departments (editorial, sound, colour, VFX) come late.
3. For an AI film, name the real roles honestly: for example "AI video generation direction", "AI-generated performances", "voice performance by [consented human name]", and tools as required by licences.
4. Music: composer, music supervisor, then each licensed track with the exact wording the licence demands (title, writers, performers, publisher, label, society where required).
5. Acknowledgements: locations, special thanks, funders and partners.
6. Legal: copyright notice, year, rights statement, disclaimers (fictional characters, AI-generated content statement).
7. Logos: production, financing, distribution.
Placement of cast versus crew can vary; the approved billing block is the source of truth.

## 6. End credits: format and timing
- Scroll speed: readable for a person reading each name once; practical test: watch at TV size and read every name (E).
- Typography: one sans serif or a clean serif, 2 weights, 2 sizes; role in light, name in medium; centre-split (role right-aligned, name left-aligned) or fully centred; consistent leading.
- Safe area: titles inside 90 percent of the frame; minimum cap height about 3 to 4 percent of frame height for 1080p (E).
- Background: black or a darkened plate; give the music its own cue and a defined end.
- Cards and rolls: render a PNG sequence or one tall image scrolled by code for exact timing; subtitle burn tools are not credit tools.
- Length: a feature roll is typically 2 to 6 minutes; shorts 20 to 60 s; platforms may ask for a minimum credit length or a skip marker (A: check the platform).
- Indic names: show in the film's primary script and a Latin transliteration if the audience needs it; keep the scheme consistent with the subtitles (indic-languages subtitles-and-transliteration.md).
- Stingers after the credits: confirm the distributor accepts them.

## 7. Credit check
Spelling, role names, order against the billing block, rights lines, music lines, logo files from the owner (not recreated), copyright year, AI-use line, accessibility lines if CBFC or a platform requires them (A: confirm), and a diff of names against the consent ledger.
