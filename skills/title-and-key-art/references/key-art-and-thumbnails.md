# Key art, posters and thumbnails

Labels: (F) source, (3P) secondary, (E) estimate, (A) assumption.

## 1. One-sheet anatomy (3P poster guides, rules of thumb, not standards)
- Structure: one strong image, a title treatment in the lower third, a compressed billing block along the bottom, each in its own band. Hierarchy: hero image, title, tagline, credit block.
- Art field about 60 to 65 percent of the height (E, one vendor's heuristic). Focal point on a third; centred suits drama, off-centre suits action and tension.
- Tagline: one line, above the title.
- Title: readable at thumbnail size and able to stand alone; it sets the width of everything beneath it.
- Billing block: small, aligned, condensed, never stretched. Credit titles ("Directed by") at least half the size of the names (3P, calculator guidance; verify against contracts). Cast order is contractual.
- Bottom strip: rating mark, logos, release date, URL or QR.
- Sizes: 27x40 in theatrical, 24x36 frame, 300 dpi print, bleed per printer (A: ask the printer for Indian standard sizes).

## 2. Key art process (cheapest first)
1. One-sentence idea: what the viewer feels and the question they ask.
2. Thumbnail sketches: 6 small compositions in grey (0 credits), choose 2.
3. Plate: generate the hero image with Higgsfield image models at draft tier. Prompt for composition and negative space where type will sit (top or bottom third). Characters come from the film's locked reference set (film-studio bibles-and-continuity.md) so the poster matches the picture. No text in the prompt.
4. People: photoreal-humans and asian-human-reference for faces, skin tone stated and never lightened; original characters or consented likeness only.
5. Final once at full resolution, upscale the approved frame only (quote and the user's yes first).
6. Type in code or a design tool: title treatment first, then tagline, billing block, then logos supplied by owners.
7. Variants: portrait 2:3, landscape 16:9, square 1:1, story 9:16; text-free versions of each; character-only and logo-only layers.
8. Localised versions for each language title (Tamil, Hindi and so on) with the Indic type rules; native check.

## 3. Style by genre (E)
Drama: restrained palette, face or silhouette, serif or humanist type. Thriller: dark, high contrast, single light, condensed sans. Romance: warm, soft, script or light serif accents. Action: off-centre, diagonal, bold sans, strong colour contrast. Comedy: bright, flat colour, playful type. Horror: negative space, small hero, thin or distressed type. Animation family: saturated, character at centre, rounded type. Keep one family; do not mix.

## 4. Thumbnails: YouTube and OTT
- YouTube: 1280x720 (16:9), JPG PNG or GIF, under 2 MB, minimum width 640 (3P; one source cites device-dependent limits, check YouTube Help). Custom thumbnails need a verified channel.
- Design: one face or object, big and close; 3 to 5 words at most; high contrast; keep the bottom-right clear because the timestamp covers it; test at about 168x94 px.
- Honest: the thumbnail must show something in the film; no fake results, no misleading before-and-after, no cosmetic or health claim without substantiation.
- OTT art: portrait 2:3 (or 3:4) hero, 16:9 landscape, logo-free and text-free variants, safe area for platform overlays; exact sizes, formats and logo rules come from the platform or aggregator spec (A, shared on onboarding).
- Festival and press: 3 to 6 key stills with captions at 300 dpi, a poster, a one-sheet; headshots supplied by the people themselves.
- Social: 9:16 cover with the title in the central safe zone; 1:1 carousel of 3 to 5 stills.
- A/B: YouTube offers thumbnail testing (A: confirm availability in Studio); change one variable at a time.

## 5. Title lettering
Options: type only; type over image; custom lettering. Draw custom lettering in vector or code, never request it from an image generator. Check the typeface licence covers film, advertising and merchandise (A: many fonts need an extended licence).

## 6. Checks
Name spelling, billing order against the contract, all logos supplied by their owners, rating mark only after certification (CBFC mark rules: confirm), release date correct, no stock or third-party imagery without a licence, AI-use statement if platforms require it, safe areas, colour proof on phone and in print, text-free versions archived.
