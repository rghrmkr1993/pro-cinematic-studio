# Director and tradition styles (techniques only)
Labels: (K) craft, (3P) third-party. Rule: describe the technique, not the person, title, character or franchise. Do not prompt for a named living artist's film, a specific character, costume or set from a protected work, or a real actor's likeness. Use style recipes below, and let the story and brand stay original. (H) Naming a style tradition in neutral terms is fine; naming a film to copy a scene is not.

## 1. Symmetrical pastel whimsy
Technique (3P, Cooke interview with Robert Yeoman and film-school summaries): camera dead-center and perpendicular to flat back walls (planimetric), centered subjects, curated pastel palette from set and costume, deadpan blocking, lateral dolly and whip pans between tableaux, anamorphic or storybook scale, soft even light.
Prompt: "Planimetric wide shot, camera perpendicular to the back wall, perfect symmetry, pastel pink, mint and butter yellow palette, flat soft light, deadpan characters standing in a row, lateral dolly, storybook diorama feel, 4:3 or 2.39:1."
Risks: models bend symmetry; specify "level horizon, mirrored left and right". Use for light brand comedy, hospitality, fashion.

## 2. Gritty handheld realism
Technique: handheld camera at eye level, available and practical light, 28 to 35mm, no zoom hits, imperfect framing, focus hunting allowed, grain, desaturated, long takes, natural sound. Deakins notes preferring handheld without a zoom to feel presence in a space (3P).
Prompt: "Handheld documentary camera, 35mm, subtle sway, available window light, slightly desaturated, 16mm grain, imperfect framing, no stabilization, ambient noise."
Keep tremor subtle; heavy shake jitters detail in Runway tests (3P).

## 3. Epic wide landscape
Technique: anamorphic wide, tiny humans in huge land, long slow crane and drone reveals, low sun, deep focus, deep staging, large format, orchestral scale, restrained palette of earth and sky.
Prompt: "Extreme wide anamorphic 2.39:1, tiny rider crossing vast dunes at low sun, long shadows, crane rising to reveal the horizon, deep focus, ochre and teal sky."
Models: Veo and Seedance handle aerial reveals well (3P).

## 4. Neon-noir
Technique: night exterior, wet pavement, saturated magenta and cyan neon spill, hard backlight, haze, silhouettes, low angles, shallow focus on lights, reflective glass, slow push-ins, synth pads.
Prompt: "Neon-noir night street after rain, magenta and cyan signs reflecting on wet asphalt, volumetric haze, lone figure in silhouette, slow push-in, anamorphic flares, deep blacks."
Avoid: cluttered brand logos; text on signs garbles, so state "unreadable signage".

## 5. Long-take immersion
Technique: one continuous camera path with choreography, camera as participant, steadicam or gimbal, depth blocking, hidden cuts at dark or whip moments, natural light so the world is 360 degrees.
Prompt: "One continuous unbroken tracking shot, steadicam follows the character through the kitchen, into the dining room and out to the garden, the world keeps moving around them, natural light, no cuts."
AI: clip length limits (4 to 30 s by model); make the oner by chaining clips with last frame as first frame and hiding joins in whip or dark pass. (V, H)

## 6. Intimate observational drama
Technique: long lens, shallow focus, still camera, lingering close-ups, dim practicals, quiet silences, restrained color.
Prompt: "Static medium close-up, 85mm, f/2, subject looks off-camera, window light, muted palette, long held beat."

## 7. Hyper-kinetic music-video style
Technique: fast cuts, fisheye, whip pans, flashes, saturated color, dutch angles, speed ramps.
Prompt: "Fisheye close-ups, whip pans, strobing light, saturated magenta, quick snap zooms, speed ramp to slow-motion on the beat."
Put the energy in the edit, one fast element per shot (V Higgsfield).

## 8. Luxury commercial
Technique: slow glide, macro, dark neutral backdrop, controlled rim light, premium sound, liquid or fabric in slow motion.
Prompt: "Slow macro glide across the surface, gold rim light, dark gradient background, droplet in 120fps slow motion, shallow focus."

## Indian cinema traditions (technique summaries, mostly 3P; regional generalizations carry a source or a no-source marker)
Caveat: each industry is diverse. The splits below describe tendencies. Sources are interviews and encyclopedic summaries (Scroll interview with Santosh Sivan, Behindwoods, ASC interview as summarized, Wikipedia profiles). Describe techniques; do not copy scenes.

### Mani Ratnam-style rain and light
Technique (3P): backlit rain against dark backgrounds, strong rim and contre-jour, sun-simulating light from mirrors and tungsten when real sun is unavailable (Sivan describes this on one collaboration), saturated nature, warm and cool contrast, silhouettes in doorways, lyrical movement, poetic use of weather, mobile cameras.
Prompt: "Night rain, heavy backlit droplets lit by a single warm lamp behind, wet reflective ground, two silhouettes in a doorway, slow lyrical tracking, warm amber against deep blue, mist."
Notes: rain is hard for models; ask for "rain backlit against dark wall" so droplets read, and keep the subject sheltered.

### Rajamouli-style epic scale
Technique (3P): mythic hero framing, low-angle hero moments, grand wide landscapes, slow motion on heroic beats, big crowd staging, sweeping crane and drone, high-contrast saturated color, heightened action through speed ramps; the cinematographer for these films has described a classical approach with large soft light sources at night and a single camera (American Cinematographer interview as summarized in 3P).
Prompt: "Mythic low-angle hero shot, wide anamorphic, towering waterfall behind, crowd below, sweeping crane reveal, slow motion on the leap, saturated gold and teal, dust and embers."

### Tamil commercial and new-wave
- Commercial: bold hero entry (low angle, slow motion, backlight, wind), glossy color, stylish fast-cut action, song picturization with saturated sets.
- New wave and realism: rooted rural or urban locations, natural light, handheld, earthy palette, observational, sound-led.
Prompt (realist): "Natural-light village street, handheld 35mm, earthy palette, hazy afternoon, observational framing".
Prompt (commercial): "Hero entry, low angle, slow-motion walk, backlit dust, bold red and teal grade, wide lens."

### Malayalam naturalistic look
Technique (3P): natural light, real interiors and landscapes, restrained grade, subtle palettes, long takes, observational blocking, authentic domestic detail (Kumbalangi Nights described as mostly natural light and landscapes while holding a fairy-tale aura).
Prompt: "Naturalistic domestic interior in coastal Kerala-style house, soft window light, green-tinted palette, handheld observational 35mm, backwater at dusk visible through the door."
Cultural settings need respectful, accurate detail; check with the user or a local reference (see `asian-human-reference`).

### Bollywood glossy and song sequences
Technique (3P): Santosh Sivan is credited with rich colour and dynamic composition across Malayalam, Tamil and Hindi work, and P. C. Sreeram with sparing, motivated light (Wikipedia profiles, Indian Film Institute) [verified en.wikipedia.org Santosh Sivan and P. C. Sreeram, 2026-10-14]. The Bollywood song-sequence recipe: large lighting teams, polished skin, wide expansive sets, saturated color and rich costume, crane and steadicam choreography, wind in fabric, slow motion, symmetrical dance formations, multi-location dream sequences. [no public source as of 2026-10-14 for a measured saturation or lighting comparison between Hindi and southern industries; checked: Wikipedia profiles, nettv4u, IMDb lists, Indian Film Institute, Behindwoods; treat the glossy-versus-realist split as a tendency]
Prompt: "Glossy song sequence, wide crane over a dancing ensemble in colorful costumes, backlit wind in fabric, rich saturated color, steadicam glide, soft glam light."

### Parallel and art cinema (K)
Technique: location natural light, handheld, minimal lighting, long observation (Satyajit Ray's location work and Govind Nihalani's handheld realism are cited as references in 3P).

## Using styles safely
- Blend 2 techniques to avoid pastiche (for example pastel symmetry with rain backlight).
- Prefer technique words over names. If a model ignores them, add 1 or 2 concrete visual details.
- Never put a real person's name or a protected character in a prompt as a way to get a look.
- Brand use: for a client, mix style recipe with the client's own palette and product; do not import another client's brand. (H)
