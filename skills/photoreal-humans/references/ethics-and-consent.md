# Ethics, consent and disclosure (researched 2026-10-13)
Not legal advice. Labels: (V) official text, (3P) law-firm, news or blog summary, (U) unverified or sources conflict. Rules move monthly: confirm the primary text and have the client's counsel or compliance owner sign off before publishing. Facts below were read from search summaries on 2026-10-13 and need primary-source checks before any legal reliance.

## 1. Real-person likeness and voice
- Written consent first. It names the person, the AI generation use, the media, the channels, the term, the territory, the voice, the right to withdraw, payment, and whether edits and digital replicas are allowed. Keep a consent log with copies.
- No celebrities, public figures, doctors, politicians or influencers without a signed licence. No "lookalike" prompts. No private individuals scraped from social media.
- Soul ID, Elements, face swap and voice cloning only for people who consented to exactly that use. Delete training photos and the model when the licence ends if the contract says so.
- Deceased persons: need the estate's licence in many jurisdictions (U); avoid.
- India: courts, mostly the Delhi High Court, have in 2026 granted urgent orders against AI deepfakes, voice clones and unconsented use of name, image and likeness, grounded in privacy and passing off rather than a single statute (3P, several law-firm and news summaries). Parody, news and criticism are generally protected (3P). Practical rule: treat any recognisable real person as consent-required.
- US: the NO FAKES Act (digital replica right with notice and takedown) was advanced by a Senate committee in June 2026 and was not shown as enacted in the sources read (3P, U); several states already have digital-replica or right-of-publicity laws. Written licences for a defined term are the safe pattern.
- Never make intimate, sexual, defamatory or humiliating content of any real person, and never remove clothing or alter bodies of real people. No non-consensual intimate imagery, ever.
- Impersonation: no fake endorsements, no cloned voices of executives, no fake news clips, no political deepfakes, no fabricated quotes.
- Employees, models and customers appearing in company content: separate release, with AI scope stated; people may refuse a synthetic version.

## 2. Synthetic (invented) people
Allowed with labelling and honest use. Rules: not a copy of a real person (check for accidental resemblance, run reverse-image searches on hero faces); a persona bible; never presented as a real customer, doctor, expert or reviewer; no invented lived experience ("it feels silky on my skin") for a synthetic persona (3P reading of ASCI, U); brand owns rights and keeps a disclosure log; prefer a real consenting spokesperson plus controlled AI variants.

## 3. No fake testimonials and no AI "results"
- No fabricated customers, doctors, dermatologists, before and after photos, reviews, star ratings, case studies or "real results" generated or retouched by AI.
- Before and after imagery: real, consented, same light and angle, unretouched beyond disclosed non-distorting edits, time span stated, typical-result language reviewed by the qualified team.
- ASCI (final AI guidelines announced 29 Sep 2026, effective about three months later, so late December 2026; 3P, U on date): fabricated testimonials, unauthorised likeness or voice, and deepfakes remain prohibited even with a label; label AI-generated influencers, digital likenesses and fabricated product settings when absence of disclosure could mislead; draft labels like "created using AI" or "enhanced using AI"; virtual influencers need a prominent and persistent not-a-real-person disclosure plus the usual paid-content disclosure (3P). Read primary text at ascionline.in.
- Cosmetic claims (smoothing, brightening, pore reduction, ageing, tone change) need substantiation and regulatory review by the qualified team; AI or retouched skin must not imply the claim. Product texture shown must match the real product.

## 4. Minors
- Absolute: no sexualised depiction of anyone who is or appears to be under 18; no ambiguous youthful characters in intimate, suggestive, swimwear or glamour content. If a prompt could be read as a minor, rewrite to a clear adult with mature features (house rule) and stop if unsure.
- No minors (real or synthetic, apparent age under 18) in beauty, cosmetic, anti-ageing, skin-result or body-image advertising, and no AI models styled as children to sell cosmetics.
- Real children: no AI generation or face training from photos of real children without verifiable guardian consent, and avoid it for marketing altogether; never put a child in a synthetic testimonial.
- Child safety reporting duties apply if illegal content is encountered; stop and escalate to the Manager.

## 5. Disclosure by platform and law
| Where | Rule (3P summaries, verify primary) | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Meta (Instagram, Facebook) | "AI info" label from C2PA and IPTC metadata plus classifiers; creators must use the disclosure tool for photorealistic video or realistic audio that was digitally created or altered; extra disclosure rules for social-issue and political ads; an optional AI-creator account label exists (May 2026) | Toggle the AI disclosure; keep metadata; do not strip it |
| YouTube | Disclose meaningfully altered or synthetic realistic content (a real person saying or doing something they did not, altered real events, realistic scenes that did not happen); exempt: scripts, captions, obviously unrealistic content, beauty filters, minor edits; since 27 May 2026 photorealistic AI gets a label under the player or an overlay on Shorts; YouTube can apply the label itself; non-disclosure risks removal or monetisation penalties | Answer the altered-content question truthfully |
| ASCI (India) | see section 3; AI label when it materially affects consumers | Add "Video created using AI" style label, persistent for virtual influencers |
| India IT Rules amendments (in force from February 2026, 3P) | platforms must label AI-generated or significantly edited content; a proposed April 2026 rule for continuous visible video labels was not final (U) | Label at source; watch for final rule |
| EU AI Act Article 50 | Transparency duties applied from 2 August 2026 (3P; sources conflict on any grace period for content already on the market, U). Deployers of systems that generate or manipulate deepfakes must disclose that content is artificial; providers must mark outputs in a machine-readable way and deployers must not strip watermarks; the creative or artistic carve-out gives only light disclosure and the Commission's guidelines treat it very narrowly for advertising; relevant date for image, audio, video is generation; applies to content used in the EU even if posted from elsewhere (3P) | Label EU-facing ads as AI-generated or manipulated; keep C2PA or SynthID marks; ask counsel on edge cases |
| Other platforms (TikTok, LinkedIn, X) | have their own AI labels; TikTok ads are not used for India work (house rule) | check each platform's current policy |
Make labels visible, not buried: in-frame text or an icon for video (a few seconds minimum in the EU draft code, 3P), caption line for images.

## 6. Provenance and records
- Keep generation metadata: model, prompt, seed, date, tool, who approved. Keep C2PA and platform labels; do not scrub them.
- Asset log fields: person or persona, consent reference, label applied, claims reviewed by, reviewer, date. Store consent PDFs outside the public repo.
- Higgsfield terms: outputs are ours with commercial use, but the vendor may use content to improve models; never upload unreleased formulations, confidential packs, customer data, or others' photos without consent (see higgsfield-master compliance.md).

## 7. Representation ethics
- Show real range of skin tone, age, body type and hair texture; do not lighten, narrow or "fix" features; do not use skin-lightening cues; avoid caricature, exoticism and pan-Asian generic faces (see asian-human-reference skill for regional detail).
- Do not use darker skin or hyperpigmentation as a "before" state or as a problem shown to sell a product unless the claim is real, substantiated and respectful.
- Colourist framing (fair equals beautiful, successful or educated) is out; check batches for depth distribution.

## 8. Gate before publishing (every job with a realistic person)
1. Real or synthetic? If real: consent on file for this use. If synthetic: not a lookalike; labelled.
2. Any child or youthful-looking subject in a prohibited context? Stop.
3. Any testimonial, doctor, review, before and after? Real and documented, or removed.
4. Any skin result or texture claim? Substantiation and review by the qualified team.
5. Label decided per platform and region; metadata kept.
6. Skin tone and features unaltered from the brief; QA score recorded.
7. Manager sign-off for any outward publication; no posting or spending without the user's explicit yes.
